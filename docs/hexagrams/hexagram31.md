# Hexagram 31 - Influence (Hsien)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Lake over Mountain, illustrates the dynamic of mutual attraction between the masculine and feminine principles. When the male, symbolizing strength, positions himself to support and elevate the female, represented by receptivity, this act of subordination from below evokes joy in her, prompting her to follow. This initial act of consideration by the strong towards the weak is what allows for the potential of new beginnings or relationships.

### Judgement
Influence. Success. Perseverance furthers. To take a maiden to wife brings good fortune.

Influence here is seen as the essence of positive social interaction. It's embodied by someone who combines outward joy with inner discipline, using happiness not to excess but appropriately, ensuring it does not turn into obsession or neglect.

### The Image
A lake on the mountain: the image of Influence. Thus the superior man encourages people to approach him by his readiness to receive them.

Those seeking knowledge or assistance will find what they need if they approach with a joyful and receptive attitude, much like the lake at the mountain's top. The lake does not protrude but instead receives; similarly, one who wields influence does so not by imposing but by being open and ready to accept help or ideas, fostering a reciprocal relationship where both parties can grow or succeed.

- Conjoining

### Key Terms
- **Influence**:
  - Contact, conjoin, move
  - Excite, mobilize, trigger
  - All totally, universal, continual, entire
  - Unite, bring together parts of a previously separated whole
  - Come into conjunction, as the planets

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
The influence shows itself in the big toe.

At this stage, the influence is just beginning to be felt, akin to a sensation in the big toe—the part of the body furthest from the head, symbolizing the start of action or movement. This line indicates the very initial phase where influence is recognized but has not yet led to any significant action or change. It's a moment of potential, where the effects of influence are nascent, waiting for the right conditions or timing to manifest into something more substantial.

### Six in Second
The influence shows itself in the calves of the legs. Misfortune. Tarrying brings good fortune.

When influence is felt in the "calves of the legs," it suggests that the movement or action being influenced is not under one's full control or is driven by external forces rather than one's own will. This scenario can lead to misfortune if one simply follows this external push without reflection. However, choosing to pause, to "tarry," and not act upon this influence brings good fortune. It's a call to break free from this external sway, to halt and reassess one's direction or decision, thereby preventing potential negative outcomes by not allowing oneself to be blindly led by external influences.

### Nine in Third
The influence shows itself in the thighs. Holds to that which follows it. To continue is humiliating.

When influence is located in the "thighs," it points to the emotional or impulsive nature of decision-making, where one might be swayed by feelings rather than reason. Holding onto or following this kind of influence, which is akin to "following one's heart" without balance or forethought, leads to actions driven by passion or whim rather than wisdom. This path, if continued, results in humiliation, as acting purely on emotional impulse often leads to regrettable decisions or outcomes. It's a caution against letting temporary or intense feelings dictate one's actions, emphasizing the need for moderation and reflection.

### Nine in Fourth
Perseverance brings good fortune. Remorse disappears. If a man is agitated in mind, and his thoughts go hither and thither, only those friends on whom he fixes his conscious thoughts will follow.

This line suggests that when one exercises influence with restraint and consistency, it leads to beneficial outcomes, allowing for the rectification of past mistakes and the disappearance of regret. However, if one's mind is unsettled, with thoughts darting from one thing to another, the influence they exert will only attract those who are directly impacted by their fleeting focus. This emphasizes the power of directed, stable intention in influence; only those who are specifically targeted by one's focused attention will respond, while scattered or erratic use of influence will not yield lasting or broad support, potentially isolating the influencer further.

### Nine in Fifth
The influence shows itself in the back of the neck. No remorse.

The "back of the neck" being the most rigid part of the body, symbolizes a point of influence that is firm, clear, and only moves or acts when it is both able and appropriate to do so. When influence is exercised in such a controlled and deliberate manner, the outcomes are free from regret or negative consequences. This line highlights the virtue of using influence with restraint and precision, ensuring that actions taken are well-considered and timed, thereby leading to results that one can stand behind without remorse.

### Six in Sixth
The influence shows itself in the jaws, cheek, and tongue.

This line directs attention to the influence wielded through speech and expression, symbolized by the parts of the face involved in speaking. It underscores the importance of being mindful about how and when one uses their words. The right words, spoken with the correct intent by the appropriate individuals at the opportune moment, can lead to significant, though sometimes unpredictable, effects. This line serves as a reminder of the power of communication and the responsibility that comes with it, where influence can be as potent as action when it comes to shaping outcomes or relationships.
