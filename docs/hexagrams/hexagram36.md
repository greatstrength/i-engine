# Hexagram 36 - Darkening of the Light (Ming I)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram, with Earth above Fire, is the antithesis of progress, symbolizing a period where the light has been obscured by the earth, leading to a state of confusion and lack of clarity. In human terms, it might reflect a leadership that lacks competence, causing uncertainty and anxiety among the populace. It calls for resilience and inner strength to navigate through this darkness while maintaining one's integrity.

### Judgement
Darkening of the Light. In adversity it furthers one to be persevering.

In such times of darkness, perseverance is crucial. One should adopt an outwardly receptive attitude towards the ambiguity of the times, not clinging to any particular outcome or direction, yet preserving an inner clarity and resolve.

### The Image
The light has sunk into the earth: the image of Darkening of the Light. Thus does the superior man live with a great mass: he veils his light, yet still shines.

It's advisable during these periods to keep a low profile, engaging less with the external world due to the prevalence of confusion and the allowance of injustice. One should be cautious in speech and maintain a strong, silent inner resolve, akin to a light that, though hidden, continues to shine. This approach helps in navigating through the darkness with integrity and wisdom, waiting for the return of light or clarity.

### Secondary Names
- Hiding Brightness

### Key Terms
- **Light**:
  - The light from fire, the sun, moon, and stars
  - Consciousness, awareness, intelligence, understanding
  - Illuminate, distinguish clearly
  - Lucid, clear, evident
  - A bright bird, the golden pheasant
- **Hide**:
  - Keep out of sight
  - Distant, remote
  - Raze, lower, level
  - Ordinary, plain, colorless
  - Cut, wound, destroy, exterminate
  - Barbarians, strangers, vulgar, uncultured people
  - A man armed with a bow

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Darkening of the light during flight. He lowers his wings. The superior man does not eat for three days on his wanderings. But he has somewhere to go. The host has occasion to gossip about him.

This line paints a picture of someone navigating through adversity or confusion, symbolized by "darkening of the light during flight." The imagery of lowering one's wings suggests a moment of retreat or caution, acknowledging the current conditions aren't favorable for advancement. The mention of not eating for three days signifies the hardships faced during this journey but also the determination to move forward despite the lack of immediate support or sustenance. Although this endeavor does not immediately succeed, the individual persists with an inner resolve and a clear sense of purpose or destination. However, this perseverance does not go unnoticed or unremarked upon, as there's mention of gossip from the host, indicating that one's actions or situation might be the subject of speculation or criticism. Yet, the focus here is on the internal strength and direction, underscoring that true progress in times of adversity is measured by one's ability to remain true to their path, undeterred by external noise or failure.

### Six in Second
Darkening of the light injures him in the left thigh. He gives aid with the strength of a horse. Good fortune.

This line speaks to someone in a position of support or subordination who faces challenges in assisting those they lead or serve, symbolized by an injury to the "left thigh." This injury might represent a limitation or obstruction in one's capacity to act or move forward in their role. Despite this hindrance, the individual harnesses an inner strength or resourcefulness, likened to the "strength of a horse," to still provide aid to others. This determination to help, even under adverse conditions, leads to positive outcomes or good fortune. It highlights the virtue of continuing to serve or support others despite personal setbacks or limitations, showcasing resilience and the ability to turn adversity into an opportunity for good.

### Nine in Third
Darkening of the light during the hunt in the south. Their great leader is captured. One must not expect perseverance too soon.

This line describes a scenario where, in the midst of navigating through or confronting the confusion or darkness, symbolized by the "hunt in the south," one encounters the central source of the problem, the "great leader." Capturing or overcoming this figure represents a significant victory or resolution to the immediate crisis. However, the text cautions against expecting quick recovery or immediate stability after such an event. Even after removing an incompetent or harmful leader, the lingering effects of their influence, including the policies they've enacted and the cultural shifts they've induced, require time to undo or rectify. This line underscores the importance of patience and long-term commitment to healing and rebuilding, recognizing that the aftermath of such leadership can be complex and enduring, necessitating a prolonged effort to truly dispel the "darkening of the light."

### Six in Fourth
He penetrates the left side of the belly. One gets at the very heart of the darkening of the light, and leaves the gate and courtyard.

This line illustrates a scenario where one, by yielding or adapting to the situation, manages to delve deeply into the core of the problem, symbolized by penetrating "the left side of the belly." This act of getting to the "very heart of the darkening of the light" indicates a profound understanding or confrontation with the root cause of the darkness or confusion. However, upon realizing that there is no feasible solution or that the situation is beyond redemption, the individual chooses to exit, metaphorically "leaving the gate and courtyard." This departure is done without hindrance, suggesting a wise retreat from a situation where further involvement would yield no positive outcome. It's a call to recognize when to disengage from fruitless or harmful scenarios, preserving one's energy and integrity for more constructive endeavors.

### Six in Fifth
Darkening of the light as with Prince Chi. Perseverance furthers.

In this line, one is depicted as being ensnared within the depths of darkness or confusion, akin to the historical figure Prince Chi, who faced significant adversity yet maintained his integrity. Here, the individual is bound by duty or circumstances that they cannot escape from, finding themselves in a situation where external flight or change is not possible. The only viable course of action is to strengthen one's inner resolve and maintain perseverance. This inward fortitude becomes the key to navigating through or surviving the period of darkness, suggesting that even when external conditions are bleak, maintaining one's moral compass and inner strength can lead to eventual betterment or resolution.

### Six in Sixth
Not light but darkness. First he climbed up to heaven, then he plunged into the depths of the earth.

This line can be seen as depicting the journey of a Sage. Initially, the Sage ascends to the heights of understanding or enlightenment, "climbing up to heaven," where he grasps the profound truths or the "way" of the universe. However, instead of immediately applying this wisdom to influence or lead, he chooses to descend back into obscurity, "plunging into the depths of the earth." This retreat is not out of defeat but from a strategic choice to wait for the right moment to act. The Sage understands that darkness, or the absence of light, has its time and place in the cycle of life, and by withdrawing his light, he allows for the natural unfolding of events without interference until the time is ripe for him to re-emerge. This act of plunging into the earth symbolizes a period of gestation, self-reflection, or preservation of wisdom, ensuring that when he does act, it will be with the full power and timing of heaven's mandate, not prematurely or in opposition to the natural order.
