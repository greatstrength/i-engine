# Hexagram 13 - Fellowship with Men (T'ung Jên)

## Overview

### Summary
In times of fellowship, Heaven above Fire blends strength with clarity, uniting diverse flames under a shared sky. One builds success through open alliances, navigating great changes with perseverance. From gate to meadow, the path shifts—initial unity falters with clannish strife or suspicion, yet external threats and struggles forge lasting bonds. Order and purpose, sustained through trials, yield harmony without remorse.

### Judgement
Fellowship with Men in the open. Success. It furthers one to cross the great water. The perseverance of the superior man furthers.

In times of fellowship, one unites openly with others, achieving success through shared purpose. Strength in this alliance furthers crossing the great water, navigating profound change. Perseverance ensures progress, sustaining collective effort toward a clear goal.

### The Image
Heaven together with fire: the image of Fellowship with Men. Thus the superior man organizes the clans and makes distinctions between things.

In times of fellowship, Heaven above Fire unites strength with clarity, illuminating shared aims. One ought to organize efforts distinctly, assigning roles to harness each contribution. By fostering order over chaos, one strengthens the collective, ensuring a formidable alliance for success.

### Key Terms
- **Fellowship**:
  - Harmonize, bring together, unite
  - Union, concord, harmony
  - Equalize, assemble, share, agree
  - Together, held in common
  - The same time and place

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Fellowship with men at the gate. No blame.

In times of fellowship, one joins others at the gate, beginning with open intent. Free of past burdens, this fresh start aligns aims clearly, incurring no blame. Such unity sets a natural foundation for collective effort.

### Six in Second
Fellowship with men in the clan. Humiliation.

In times of fellowship, one limits bonds to a clan, fostering narrow ties within the broader group. This factionalism breeds humiliation when exposed, undermining unity. If unchecked, such division spreads, tarnishing the collective purpose with disgrace.

### Nine in Third
He hides weapons in the thicket. He climbs the high hill in front of it. For three years he does not rise up.

In times of fellowship, one harbors distrust, concealing weapons in the thicket and watching from a high hill. This defensive stance, persisting three years, stalls progress amid suspicion. Caution delays unity, preserving tension over action.

### Nine in Fourth
He climbs up on his wall; he cannot attack. Good fortune.

In times of fellowship, one mounts the wall, facing an external threat rather than internal foes. Unable to strike within, this shift averts conflict. Good fortune arises as allies unite against the outer peril, reinforcing collective purpose through timely defense.

### Nine in Fifth
Men bound in fellowship first weep and lament, but afterward they laugh. After great struggles they succeed in meeting.

In times of fellowship, one faces initial strife, marked by weeping and lament. Yet perseverance through these trials unites the group, turning sorrow to joy. Success follows great struggles, as shared effort forges a lasting bond.

### Nine in Sixth
Fellowship with men in the meadow. No remorse.

In times of fellowship, one gathers in the meadow, a natural convergence free of strife. This effortless unity, aligned with nature’s flow, incurs no remorse. Strengthened by harmony, the group thrives without regret.
