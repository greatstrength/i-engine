# Hexagram 63 - After Completion (Chi Chi)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Water over Fire, shows the conditions for a time of climax, a fragile equilibrium between Peace and Stagnation where all parts—firm lines in odd places and yielding lines in even places—are perfectly arranged. This delicate balance requires vigilant care, as any unchecked disorder from without can disrupt the harmony within, tipping the order into chaos.

### Judgement
After completion. Success in small matters. Perseverance furthers. At the beginning good fortune, at the end disorder.

The transition to a new time has been accomplished. An equilibrium is in place, but ceaseless vigilance and addressing small but critical matters is important to ensure stability. Otherwise, the orderly situation shall devolve into chaos.

### The Image
Water over fire: the image of the condition in After Completion. Thus the superior man takes thought of misfortune and arms himself against it in advance.

One ought to understand the gravity of negative repercussions from the completion of a work and arm themselves in advance.

### Secondary Names
- None

### Key Terms
- **Already**:
  - Completed, finished
  - Mark of the past tense
  - Thus, that is being so
  - A person having already been seated and beginning the meal
- **Ford**:
  - Cross a river, overcome an obstacle, begin an action
  - Give help, bring relief
  - Succeed, bring to a successful conclusion, complete
  - Water running over the flat bottom of a shallow fording place

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
He brakes his wheels. He gets his tail in the water. No blame.

A new time has come, but it is not yet bound to last for much longer. Should one see the toxic nature of the situation ahead of time, they may free themselves from ill consequences.

### Six in Second
The woman loses the curtain of her carriage. Do not run after it; on the seventh day you will get it.

The inertia of success from the times leaves one who wishes to embark on an undertaking without support. One ought to be patient, as inertia shall inevitably be set into motion.

### Nine in Third
The Illustrious Ancestor disciplines the Devil's Country. After three years he conquers it. Inferior people must not be employed.

Once a new time has arrived and the dust settles, one is driven to expand outwardly into the unknown. It may take much time before the chaos from this expansion is tamed and brought to peace. Lesser inclinations may not be introduced in any way.

### Six in Fourth
The finest clothes turn to rags. Be careful all day long.

After the onset of a new time, difficulties emerge as one seeks to maintain the new equilibrium. While others may be apt to forget these transitory difficulties, one ought to best remember and prepare themselves for future trouble.

### Nine in Fifth
The neighbor in the east who slaughters an ox does not attain as much real happiness as the neighbor in the west with his small offering.

Forms, customs, and habits are always subject to change during a new time. One finds that many forms of devotion have become superficial, preferring simplicity in their devotion.

### Six in Sixth
He gets his head in the water. Danger.

One takes pause to admire the coming of a new time. This inertia presents danger, as the situation has not yet stabilized.
