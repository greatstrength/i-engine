# Hexagram 35 - Progress (Chin)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram, with Fire above Earth, vividly captures the essence of the sun's ascent over the horizon, transforming the dimness of night into the vibrant light of day. This transition naturally stirs the world into motion, awakening plants, animals, and human endeavors from slumber to activity. The flow from darkness to light embodies the very image of Progress, illustrating how natural cycles of illumination can inspire and drive forward movement and growth in all aspects of life.

### Judgement
Progress. The powerful prince is honored with horses in large numbers. In a single day he is granted an audience three times.

When someone in a position of influence wields their power with wisdom, avoiding any abuse of clarity or authority, they secure the profound trust of their superiors. This trust is vividly illustrated by the honor of being bestowed with horses in large numbers—a symbol of loyalty, wisdom, and readiness for significant endeavors. The magnitude of this gesture underscores the scale and importance of the tasks they are entrusted with. Moreover, this recognition is further highlighted by being granted an audience not once, but three times in a single day, signifying deep respect, reliance on their judgment, and the critical nature of their leadership.

### The Image
The sun rises over the earth: the image of Progress. Thus the superior man himself brightens his bright virtue.

Just as the sun grows brighter as it reaches its zenith, illuminating the world more fully, so too should individuals of enduring character shine a light upon themselves. This self-illumination is a process of personal growth, where one identifies and reinforces positive behaviors while addressing those that need correction, ensuring continual progress in one's character and actions.

### Secondary Names
- Prospering

### Key Terms
- **Progress**:
  - Grow and flourish, as young plants do in the sun
  - Advance, increase, prosper
  - Be promoted, rise, go up
  - Permeate, impregnate
  - Winged creatures taking flight before dawn

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Progressing, but turned back. Perseverance brings good fortune. If one meets with no confidence, one should remain calm. No mistake.

This line speaks to a situation where there might be initial setbacks or a lack of support from others, symbolized by being "turned back." However, the key here is perseverance. By steadfastly continuing to do what is right, despite the absence of external validation or confidence from others, one can still achieve good fortune. It's crucial in such moments to maintain calmness and not let the skepticism or negativity from the outside world derail one's efforts or convictions. This approach is not a mistake but a testament to one's integrity and determination, suggesting that true progress often requires an inner belief in one's path, even when external conditions are not supportive.

### Six in Second
Progressing, but in sorrow. Perseverance brings good fortune. Then one obtains great happiness from one's ancestress.

In this scenario, one moves forward amidst personal challenges or without the expected support, leading to feelings of sorrow or isolation. However, the message is clear: do not abandon your path. Through perseverance, despite the current sadness, one will encounter good fortune. The mention of receiving great happiness from one's "ancestress" suggests that this joy or support might come from an unexpected or traditional source, often overlooked in moments of seeking aid. This line implies that sometimes, the path to happiness or assistance can be counterintuitive or come from sources we might not initially consider, like the wisdom or legacy of our ancestors, showcasing the value of patience and the unconventional sources of joy or aid in our journey of progress.

### Six in Third
All are in accord. Remorse disappears.

This line suggests a scenario where one finds themselves surrounded by like-minded individuals or in an environment where everyone's efforts or intentions are aligned with one's own. By associating with those who share and support your goals, there's no need to worry about advancing in solitude or against opposition. This unity eliminates any feelings of regret or doubt about one's path, reinforcing the idea that collective harmony can ease the journey of progress, making it less burdensome and more fruitful.

### Nine in Fourth
Progress like a hamster. Perseverance brings danger.

The mention of a hamster here invokes the image of a small, industrious creature, often preoccupied with activities that are primarily self-preserving or of minor consequence. In the context of progress, this line warns that engaging in or persisting with self-preserving activities that are petty or dubious will invite danger. As times of progress naturally bring things into the light, behaviors that are small-minded or self-serving are at risk of exposure, leading to potential harm or consequences for those who continue down such paths.

### Six in Fifth
Remorse disappears. Take not gain and loss to heart. Undertakings bring good fortune. Everything serves to further.

This line advises one to release any lingering regrets and not to be overly affected by the fluctuations of success and failure. Recognizing that both victory and defeat are natural parts of life's journey, the focus should be on persisting in righteousness. By maintaining this attitude, one's endeavors are met with good fortune, and all circumstances align to support further advancement. This perspective fosters resilience and optimism, ensuring that progress is not hindered by the emotional weight of outcomes but propelled by a commitment to what is fundamentally good and true.

### Nine in Sixth
Making progress with the horns is permissible only for the purpose of punishing one's own city. To be conscious of danger brings good fortune. No blame. Perseverance brings humiliation.

This line underscores the idea that progress sometimes necessitates self-criticism or the examination of one's own community or practices. The metaphor of using "horns" for punishment suggests a direct, perhaps harsh, approach to correction, but it's only justified when aimed at self-improvement or rectifying one's own faults. Being aware of the potential risks or backlash from such actions leads to good fortune, as it implies a measured and thoughtful approach. However, if one persists in this method out of ego or to assert dominance rather than for constructive reasons, it leads to humiliation. This is because the intent behind such actions should be to restore balance or order within oneself or one's community, not to demonstrate power or control, which could erode trust and respect.
