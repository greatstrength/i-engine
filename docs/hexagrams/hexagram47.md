# Hexagram 47 - Oppression (K'un)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Lake over Water, portrays an exhausted lake with no water to nourish the surrounding landscape, symbolizing a state of depletion and constraint. It reflects a time when a leader faces oppression, surrounded by followers in positions of power who fail to serve effectively, rendering actions fruitless. This calls for enduring fortitude, as external efforts may yield no lasting impact, urging inner resilience to navigate the challenges.

### Judgement
Oppression. Success. Perseverance. The great man brings about good fortune. No blame. When one has something to say, it is not believed.

The hexagram of Oppression portrays an exhausted lake with no water to nourish the surrounding landscape. So too is a leader oppressed by followers in positions of leadership unable to be of service. In this way, a leader ought to bear an enduring fortitude to bide the time, as their actions shall leave no lasting impacts at this time.

### The Image
There is no water in the lake: the image of Exhaustion. Thus the superior man stakes his life on following his will.

The lake that has filled gradually over time has become exhausted, as water suddenly flowed out from beneath. So too do situations and experiences gathered gradually in life change suddenly due to a natural change in circumstance. A leader with fortitude of character understands that such changes may be sudden and without warning, thus they prefer to cling to their missions and goals than their possessions.

### Secondary Names

### Key Terms
- **Oppress**:
  - Enclosed, encircled
  - Restrict, limit
  - Punishment, penal codes, prison
  - Worry, anxiety, fear
  - Fatigue, weary
  - Poverty
  - A growing tree surrounded by an enclosure

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
One sits oppressed under a bare tree and strays into a gloomy valley. For three years one sees nothing.

A leader, when faced with opposition, must first be able to overcome the trouble inwardly. Should they be incapable, they shall lament in sorrow until they are able to overcome their inner insecurity.

### Nine in Second
One is oppressed while at meat and drink. The man with the scarlet knee bands is just coming. It furthers one to offer sacrifice. To set forth brings misfortune. No blame.

A leader is oppressed while on safe turf "while at meat and drink." There is an opportunity for the leader to cast judgement via "the man with the scarlet knee bands" on the injustice, yet to set forth casting judgement brings ill outcomes at this time. Instead, the leader may offer a small token of devotion with the hopes of better outcomes without ill repercussion.

### Six in Third
A man permits himself to be oppressed by stone, and leans on thorns and thistles. He enters his house and does not see his wife. Misfortune.

A leader with no sense of self-reliance is one who is easily influenced by the opinions of others, looks for support from opportunities and/or followers that would naturally do them harm, and has no inner circle. Ill outcomes are inevitable with this leadership.

### Nine in Fourth
He comes very quietly, oppressed in a golden carriage. Humiliation, but the end is reached.

A leader returns quietly and without celebration to their followers at the behest of a Higher power, or "oppressed in a golden carriage." This return is due on account of poor decision-making by the leader, but all ends as it should.

### Nine in Fifth
His nose and feet are cut off. Oppression at the hands of the man with the purple knee bands. Joy comes softly. It furthers one to make offerings and libations.

A leader is most shamed and dishonored. Those with the power to help, as signified by the purple knee bands, choose not to. It is best for the leader to quietly accept such circumstances as they are and give strength to their inner fortitude at this time.

### Six in Sixth
He is oppressed by creeping vines. He moves uncertainly and says, "Movement brings remorse." If one feels remorse over this and makes a start, good fortune comes.

A leader is oppressed by actions performed in the past. They are irresolute to move forward, as they believe that prior conditions of movement are impacting current ones. Only when the leader can grasp the situation and readjust their attitude towards it that the oppression can be mastered.
