# Hexagram 26 - Taming Power of the Great (Ta Ch'u)

## Overview

### Summary

The Taming Power of the Great emphasizes the importance of gathering and restraining creative energies to serve a higher purpose. It advises individuals to channel their potent forces wisely, particularly in service to those in authority, preparing for significant undertakings through wisdom and discipline.

### Judgement

The Taming Power of the Great. Perseverance furthers. Not eating at home brings good fortune. It furthers one to cross the great water.

The hexagram, with the Mountain over Heaven, signifies a period where one should gather or even restrain the creative energies within themselves. This reflects the role of the great Sage, who has the capacity to manage and channel such potent forces for the benefit of all. One who possesses the power to tame great energies should seek to be of service to those in authority, readying themselves for significant undertakings.

### The Image

Heaven within the mountain: The image of the Taming Power of the Great. Thus the superior man acquaints himself with many sayings of antiquity and many deeds of the past, in order to strengthen his character thereby.

The metaphor of a treasure hidden within the mountain, symbolizing Heaven's bounty, points to the profound truths embedded in natural laws, often captured in myths, legends, and ancient teachings. By diligently engaging with these ancient texts and practices, one not only gains wisdom but also harnesses a heavenly insight or strength, which can be used to guide, influence, or transform the world in meaningful ways.

### Secondary Names

- Great Accumulating

### Key Terms

- **Great**:
  - Big, noble, important
  - Able to protect others
  - Orient one's will towards a self-imposed goal
  - The ability to lead or guide one's life
  - Yang energy
- **Gather**:
  - Accumulate, collect, take in, hoard, retain
  - Control, restrain
  - Take care of, support, tolerate
  - Tame, train or pasture animals, raise, bring up, domesticate
  - Be tamed or controlled by something
  - Soil retaining fertile black silt

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First

Danger is at hand. It furthers one to desist.

There is a desire to move forward or take action with one's great power, but the current situation presents a significant risk. It is advisable to hold back or stop, as proceeding could lead to peril. This line underscores the wisdom of restraint, recognizing when the moment for action is not favorable, and choosing to wait or retreat until conditions are more conducive to success.

### Nine in Second

The axel-trees are taken from the wagon.

In this context, even though one possesses great power or capability, they are effectively immobilized by a force or situation that is clearly superior or more restrictive, symbolized by the removal of the axel-trees from a wagon, which would prevent it from moving. This line suggests a time of waiting or patience, acknowledging that it's prudent to accept the current limitation or obstruction, understanding that now is not the time for movement or action. It reflects a scenario where one's potential to act is checked by external conditions beyond their control, advocating for a strategic pause until the obstruction is lifted or another path becomes clear.

### Nine in Third

A good horse that follows others. Awareness of danger, with perseverance, furthers. Practice chariot driving and armed defense daily. It furthers one to have somewhere to go.

This line likens someone with great power or potential to a "good horse" that follows its purpose or path diligently. It's emphasized that such an individual should be aware of the dangers that come with their capabilities and responsibilities. Perseverance in this awareness is beneficial. The advice to practice "chariot driving and armed defense daily" suggests the importance of honing one's skills, both in leadership (chariot driving) and in protecting oneself or one's interests (armed defense). This preparation is crucial because future endeavors will not be straightforward and will demand both knowledge and practical experience to navigate successfully. Thus, having a clear direction or goal ("somewhere to go") is advantageous for channeling one's efforts effectively.

### Six in Fourth

The headboard of a young bull. Great good fortune.

This line uses the metaphor of a "headboard on a young bull" to describe the first instance of restraint or guidance within the hexagram, suggesting a force or influence that is applied for the ultimate benefit of the individual. Just as a headboard might guide the growth of a bull's horns to prevent them from becoming a hazard to itself or others, this restraint is for one's overall good. It implies that such guidance or limitation at this stage allows for safe and positive development, leading to great good fortune by ensuring that one's growth or actions are directed in a way that avoids future dangers or missteps.

### Six in Fifth

The tusk of a gelded boar. Good fortune.

This line uses the imagery of a boar's tusk to convey a lesson in taming or controlling power. A boar's tusks are inherently dangerous, but if the boar itself is made docile or "gelded" (castrated), the threat of those tusks diminishes. This suggests that sometimes, to achieve a peaceful or beneficial outcome, one must address the source of potential aggression or uncontrolled energy at its root, altering its nature over time. This approach leads to good fortune by ensuring that one's power or the power of others is managed or transformed in such a way that it no longer poses a threat but instead serves a constructive or harmonious purpose.

### Nine in Sixth

One attains the way of heaven. Success.

This line signifies the pinnacle of harnessing and directing creative energies in accordance with the natural order or the "way of heaven." Here, the individual has mastered the art of using their power or influence not for personal gain but for the benefit of all, much like the heavens nourish and sustain the earth. This attainment leads to success, as one's actions are in perfect harmony with universal principles, bringing blessings and positive outcomes to the world at large. It's the ultimate expression of taming the great power within, where the individual becomes a conduit for celestial or divine benevolence.
