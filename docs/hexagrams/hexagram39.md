# Hexagram 39 - Obstruction (Chien)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Water above Mountain, depicts a perilous scenario where one faces a deep river gorge before an impassable mountain, symbolizing natural obstacles that block progress. This calls for strategic retreat and reflection rather than blind persistence, seeking wisdom to navigate life’s inevitable challenges with resilience and growth.

### Judgement
Obstruction. The southwest furthers. The northeast does not further. It furthers one to see the great man. Perseverance brings good fortune.

The hexagram features the trigrams of Water above the Mountain, depicting a scenario where one is caught between a deep river gorge and an impassable mountain, symbolizing natural obstacles that arise in life. When confronted with such a blockage, the wise choice is often to retreat rather than to forge ahead blindly. Seeking guidance from experts or individuals with greater insight ("the great man") is advised to navigate or overcome these challenges.

### The Image
Water on the mountain: the image of Obstruction. Thus the superior man turns his attention to himself and molds his character.

This hexagram underscores a core principle of the I Ching: personal growth through adversity. Facing and surmounting obstacles, particularly when aligned with the natural flow of time, offers an opportunity for character development. Through the deliberate and effective handling of these challenges, one can accumulate virtues, enhancing their inner strength and readiness for future responsibilities. This process of internalizing one's efforts and experiences serves not only to mold one's character but also to prepare for the leadership or duties that await.

### Secondary Names
- Difficulties

### Key Terms
- **Obstruction**:
  - Difficulty, obstacle, blocks, afflictions
  - Limp, walk lamely or haltingly
  - Weak, crooked, unfortunate
  - A cold foot devoid of circulation and unable to move

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Going leads to obstructions, coming meets with praise.

When faced with an obstruction, the attempt to push forward will only lead to further complications. Instead, the wise choice is to retreat to safety, regroup, and rethink one's approach. This decision to return rather than to stubbornly persist is not only prudent but also earns recognition or praise from others. Such acknowledgment strengthens relationships and builds trust, as it demonstrates wisdom, adaptability, and care for the collective well-being, thereby enhancing one's standing or support within their community or team.

### Six in Second
The king's servant is beset by obstruction upon obstruction, but it is not his own fault.

This line describes a scenario where someone, acting as a servant or agent for a higher authority, encounters one challenge after another. These obstructions are not a result of personal misjudgement or error but are instead a consequence of following the directives or serving the interests of their superior. The emphasis here is on the recognition that the individual's situation is not due to their own actions but is rather an occupational hazard of their role. This line does not provide direct guidance on how to overcome these obstacles but rather highlights the inherent difficulties of serving in such a capacity, suggesting a reflection on the nature of duty and service.

### Nine in Third
Going leads to obstructions; hence he comes back.

This line underscores the wisdom of recognizing when engagement with an obstacle is likely to yield more harm than good. Here, the individual sees that advancing against the obstruction would entail unnecessary or disproportionate sacrifices. Instead of persisting in a fruitless or costly endeavor, the wise choice is to retreat or return. This decision reflects an understanding that sometimes, the most prudent action is to avoid confrontation with a challenge that doesn't promise proportional rewards or learning. It's a lesson in discernment—knowing when to step back to conserve energy and resources for battles or opportunities that are more in line with one's capabilities or goals.

### Six in Fourth
Going leads to obstructions, coming leads to union.

This line speaks to a situation where pushing forward into the obstruction only leads to further complications. However, by choosing to retreat or return, one finds themselves in the company of others who share the same challenge or perspective on the issue at hand. This scenario highlights the principle of attraction—where one's actions, particularly in times of adversity, can naturally draw together allies or kindred spirits. It teaches the value of patience and timing, suggesting that sometimes the best approach to overcoming an obstacle is not through solitary effort but through collaboration with those who are similarly motivated or affected. By coming back, one not only avoids the pitfalls of solitary struggle but also sets the stage for a collective effort that can lead to resolution or transformation.

### Nine in Fifth
In the midst of the greatest obstructions, friends come.

This line illustrates a scenario where one takes on a significant challenge or responsibility on behalf of a superior, facing what seems like insurmountable obstacles alone at first. However, the individual's capability, likened to that of a prince, not only manages to navigate these challenges but does so with such grace and skill that it inspires admiration and support from others. This awe-inspiring performance attracts friends or allies who were previously on the sidelines, now motivated to join in the effort. The key message here is that when one is entrusted with great responsibilities, they don't necessarily need to rely solely on their own resources or strength. Instead, their approach to these duties, marked by competence and integrity, can naturally draw in assistance from those around them.

### Six in Sixth
Going leads to obstructions, coming leads to great good fortune. It furthers one to see the great man.

This line describes a situation where, instead of persisting against an obstruction, one chooses to retreat. This retreat, however, leads to an extraordinary opportunity—an encounter with a Sage who is in retirement, living incognito among the common people. Such meetings are deemed highly fortuitous, suggesting that sometimes the greatest insights or changes in one's life path come not from battling through obstacles but from stepping back and allowing fate or serendipity to guide the way. The line encourages the recognition that in moments of obstruction, the act of returning or taking a step back can open doors to profound wisdom or life-altering guidance, particularly from figures like the Sage, who might influence one's undertakings or personal growth in deeply meaningful ways.
