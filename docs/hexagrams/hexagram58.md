# Hexagram 58 - The Joyous (Tui)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of Joy, with Lake doubled, represents the Youngest Daughter, embodying the infectious pleasure of pursuing a higher purpose. It contrasts the restraint of the Mountain with the excitability of the Lake, where a yielding line atop two firm lines mirrors a strong foundation uplifting the weak, sparking joy. This state of natural flow must be tempered with restraint to avoid excess, ensuring joy fosters growth rather than mirthful debauchery.

### Judgement
The Joyous. Success. Perseverance is favorable.

Joy is infectious and brings success to wherever it spreads. Yet Joy that is left unchecked devolves into mirth and debauchery. Thus it is favorable to balance between joyous social engagement while restraining themselves in accordance with their Conduct and what is right by the times.

### The Image
Lakes resting one on the other: the image of The Joyous. Thus the superior man joins with his friends for discussion and practice.

The hexagram portrays the Lake trigram doubled. Thus when one lake begins to drain, the other lake replenishes it in a Modest way. People in society who are spiritually connected represent this image through the joy of friendship and joint experiences. One may travel far on their own, but such a journey would be ponderously one-sided.

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Contented joyousness. Good fortune.

One who finds contentment within themselves, their activities, and their community lives a life of positive developments.

### Nine in Second
Sincere joyousness. Good fortune. Remorse disappears.

One finds themselves seeking joy from followers to which one has no alignment. When one in this position resolves themselves inwardly to find joy by dissolving themselves of these followers, this willful separation leads to positive developments. Any cause for regret shall disappear.

### Six in Third
Coming joyousness. Misfortune.

A true sense of joy must spring forth from within. One who seeks joy from things beyond them prepares themselves for negative developments.

### Nine in Fourth
Joyousness that is weighed is not at peace. After ridding himself of mistakes a man has joy.

One is at conflict over choosing a source of joy, between base pleasures and higher pursuits. Once one has come to realize the danger in the former and the benefit of the latter, then joy may be found.

### Nine in Fifth
Sincerity toward disintegrating influences is dangerous.

Should one, in the pursuit of joy, invite to them disintegrating influences, they will certainly be led to danger.

### Six in Sixth
Seductive joyousness.

One has given themselves over to their base pleasures. With no will of their own, they drift at the whims of their desires.
