# Hexagram 40 - Deliverance (Hsieh)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram is formed by the trigrams of Thunder above Water, symbolizing a release from tension or a resolution to a previously volatile situation. It represents a moment where the energy of movement has overcome danger, providing an opportunity for liberation or relief. The advice here is to swiftly return to the state of normalcy before the disturbance occurred. If one's commitment to an endeavor has waned, returning to one's previous state can bring good fortune. Conversely, if one is deeply invested in their current undertaking, hastening to complete it aligns with the energies of deliverance, heralding a new era or phase.

### Judgement
Deliverance. The southwest furthers. If there is no longer anything where one has to go, return brings good fortune. If there is still something where someone has to go, hastening brings good fortune.

This hexagram advises that in times of deliverance, one should assess their commitment to their current path. Returning to a prior state brings good fortune if the endeavor no longer serves, while hastening to complete a meaningful task aligns with the energy of resolution, leading to success.

### The Image
Thunder and rain set in: the image of Deliverance. Thus the superior man pardons mistakes and forgives misdeeds.

Holding onto past grievances can obstruct one's path to future possibilities. Therefore, the superior individual recognizes the need to forgive past misdeeds, understanding these actions as products of the circumstances rather than inherent character flaws. By embracing this philosophy of forgiveness, one not only clears emotional or mental clutter but also frees up energy for new endeavors, aligning themselves with the natural flow of life where past resolutions pave the way for future actions.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Without blame.

This line signifies that a period of deliverance has arrived, where the previous tension or conflict naturally dissipates. The simplicity of this line, stating "without blame," conveys that this release of tension occurs without any fault or preconceived notions. It is a natural progression or resolution of the situation. This moment of deliverance, free from blame, offers everyone involved the chance to move forward or to use this new state of affairs for personal growth or development without the weight of past judgments or errors. It's an encouragement to embrace this opportunity for change or advancement, knowing that the circumstances themselves are not a reflection of personal shortcomings but rather a part of the natural cycle of release and renewal.

### Nine in Second
One kills three foxes in the field and receives a yellow arrow. Perseverance brings good fortune.

This line illustrates an active participation in the path to deliverance by confronting and eliminating the sources of mischief or corruption, symbolized by the "three foxes." This direct action represents tackling the root causes of tension or discord head-on. The reward for this effort is the receipt of a "yellow arrow," where yellow signifies wisdom, insight, and balance. The arrow embodies a strategy of approach that is both direct and precise, advocating for justice in addressing wrongdoing. Its acute and ranged nature suggests that the release of tension should be achieved in a way that is just, immediate, and effective, yet with enough distance to prevent unnecessary harm or entanglement. This approach highlights a sophisticated method of conflict resolution, where one can act decisively while employing wisdom to minimize personal risk and collateral damage.

This line also emphasizes the value of learning from such experiences, internalizing the art of problem-solving, and mastering the process of achieving deliverance through direct engagement with negativity. These skills are invaluable, paving the way for future opportunities and success, provided one persists in their commitment to maintaining harmony and order.

### Six in Third
If a man carries a burden on his back and nonetheless rides in a carriage, he thereby encourages robbers to draw near. Perseverance leads to humiliation.

This line addresses the scenario where an individual, unaccustomed to the privileges or status symbolized by riding in a carriage, continues to carry their burdens as if they were still on foot. This behavior can be seen as a contradiction or an inability to adapt to one's new circumstances, signaling to others that they might not truly belong in this elevated position or are unworthy of their new status. Such actions draw negative attention, inviting criticism or exploitation from those who perceive this person as vulnerable or undeserving, much like robbers drawn to an easy target.

The line warns of the dangers of not fully embracing or understanding the responsibilities and behaviors appropriate to one's new role or station during a time of deliverance. Instead of finding relief or success, one might face humiliation or setbacks because their actions betray a lack of alignment with their current situation. This emphasizes the importance of letting go of past habits or burdens when transitioning into new phases of life, ensuring that one's conduct matches their new reality to avoid attracting adversity or losing credibility.

### Nine in Fourth
Deliver yourself from your great toe. Then the companion comes, and him you can trust.

This line speaks to the necessity of letting go of relationships or connections that were once crucial but have now become outdated or limiting as one moves into a new phase of deliverance. The "great toe" metaphorically represents those foundational or supportive elements in one's life that, while once vital for balance and movement (like during times of standstill), no longer serve the same purpose in the context of new beginnings.

By making the difficult choice to "deliver" oneself from these past dependencies, one clears the path for new alliances or relationships that are more in tune with current needs or personal evolution. The arrival of a new companion, one who can be trusted, signifies that this act of release is not just about loss but about making room for more suitable connections. This transition, though possibly painful, aligns one with opportunities and relationships that better support their journey forward, emphasizing personal growth and adaptation to changing circumstances.

### Six in Fifth
If only the superior man can deliver himself, it brings good fortune. Thus he proves to inferior men that he is in earnest.

This line underscores the importance of self-deliverance or personal transformation as a precursor to wider influence or change. By focusing on overcoming one's own internal obstacles or conflicts through strong inner resolve, the superior individual not only achieves personal liberation but also sets an example for others. The release of this inner truth or authenticity has a ripple effect, positively influencing those around them.

This line reminds us that often, the most significant barriers to progress or peace lie within ourselves. By addressing these personal challenges, one not only improves their own situation but also demonstrates to those who might be less committed or insightful that their efforts are genuine and their leadership is based on self-mastery. This approach to deliverance suggests that personal growth and integrity are powerful catalysts for change, leading to good fortune by aligning one's actions with their true nature and thereby inspiring or guiding others.

### Six in Sixth
The prince shoots at a hawk on a high wall. He kills it. Everything serves to further.

This line portrays a scenario where someone of exceptional skill, symbolized by the "prince," confronts a formidable challenge, represented by the "hawk on a high wall." The hawk, being both cunning and in a position of dominance, signifies a problem or obstacle that is particularly difficult to overcome. However, the prince, with his heroic grace and unique abilities, addresses this issue directly, precisely, and from a distance, illustrating that some challenges require the full expression of one's talents without restraint.

This line encourages the understanding that when faced with a situation that matches one's unique skills or calling, there's no need for hesitation or self-limitation. The act of overcoming such a challenge not only brings deliverance but also serves to advance everything else in one's life or project. It's a call to action for those with extraordinary capabilities to step up and use their gifts fully, ensuring that their intervention leads to a broader, positive impact, aligning with the natural flow of events where everything then serves to further one's path or purpose.
