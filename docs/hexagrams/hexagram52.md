# Hexagram 52 - Keeping Still (Kên)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Mountain doubled, embodies the Youngest Son, where a firm line rises to rest atop yielding lines, signifying inner stillness. Like a mountain standing firm, it reflects a state where one’s heart finds repose, unswayed by ego’s whims. This stillness fosters clarity and authenticity, aligning one’s actions with their true character and the natural limits of the moment.

### Judgement
Keeping Still. Keeping his back still so that he no longer feels his body. He goes into his courtyard and does not see his people. No blame.

When one brings themselves to stillness, they are no longer inspired into movement through their lesser inclinations. Things that seemed familiar at one time now seem strange and foreign, but no harm comes from this. In this way, all things in nature find their limits and come to the true expression of their character.

### The Image
Mountains standing close together: the image of Keeping Still. Thus the superior man does not permit his thoughts to go beyond his situation.

One of enduring fortitude has the inner composure to not act upon every single thought. While it is impossible to silence thoughts indefinitely, one ought to listen to those that serve to benefit further.

### Key Terms
- **Still**:
  - Limit, boundary, obstacle
  - Quiet, calm, refuse to advance
  - Enclose, mark off, confine
  - Finish, complete
  - Reflect on what has come before
  - Firm, solid, simple, straightforward
  - The mountain as a limit and a refuge
  - Stop, bring to a standstill
  - A person reflecting upon the past leading to the present

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Keeping his toes still. No blame. Continued perseverance furthers.

Halting before the beginning of motion does no harm. Maintaining such a firm restraint removes one from the present situation enough to where they can see things for what they are and act accordingly.

### Six in Second
Keeping his calves still. He cannot rescue him who he follows. His heart is not glad.

One who was stirred to movement through means beyond themselves naturally comes to a rest. However, not all that was in motion with them follows suit. This makes one distraught, but there is little anyone can do at this time.

### Nine in Third
Keeping his hips still. Making his sacrum stiff. Dangerous. The heart suffocates.

Coming to stillness is a process that ought to happen naturally in accordance with the time. Should such stillness be forced on one through external or unnatural means, it only serves to bring danger to one's spirit if they are not careful.

### Six in Fourth
Keeping his trunk still. No blame.

One finds it difficult to silence the impulses of the ego, but they still manage to hold their actions in check. This shall not bear future repercussions.

### Six in Fifth
Keeping his jaws still. The words have order. Remorse disappears.

When one finds themselves in a difficult position, exercising both tactful reserve and using the appropriate words for the situation can potentially turn things around.

### Nine in Sixth
Noble-hearted keeping still. Good fortune.

One has achieved the full stillness of the heart. In this way, any energy that is acted upon is one that serves to further and benefit all things.
