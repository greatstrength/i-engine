# Hexagram 24 - Return (Fu)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram is linked to the eleventh month, from December to January, encompassing the Winter Solstice—the darkest time transitioning into the return of light, symbolizing the end of winter's harshness. It heralds a renewal, where vitality and opportunity emerge from stagnation, marking a natural cycle of resurgence and new beginnings.

### Judgement
Return. Success. Going out and coming in without error. Friends come without blame. To and fro goes the way. On the seventh day comes return. It furthers to have somewhere to go.

This return ushers in new conditions, infusing new opportunities and vitality into what was once stagnation and inertia. The transition to these new circumstances is governed by cosmic timing, not human action. Therefore, regardless of one's past journeys, encounters, or achievements, there will be a shift to a new normal, signifying a fresh start. The mention of the "seventh day" as the point of return reflects a cycle where six stages lead to a pivotal moment of change, paralleling how solstices and equinoxes are spaced six months apart, with the seventh marking a significant shift.

### The Image
Thunder within the earth: the image of the Turning Point. Thus the kings of antiquity closed the passes at the time of solstice. Merchants and strangers did not go about, and the ruler did not travel through the provinces.

Historically, the Winter Solstice was a time for rest and spiritual nourishment, a period where one should conserve energy rather than expend it prematurely. As light returns, it's crucial that this renewal leads to growth and flourishing, embodying the spirit of return not just in nature but in personal and communal life as well.

### Secondary Names
- The Turning Point

### Key Terms
- **Return**:
  - Go back, turn back, return to the starting point
  - Come back, reappear
  - Resurgence, re-birth, renaissance
  - Re-establish, renew, renovate, restore
  - Again, anew
  - An earlier time and place

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Return from a short distance. No need for remorse. Great good fortune.

Minor missteps or deviations are inevitable, but the key is to recognize and correct them before they lead one too far astray. This line advises looking back or reassessing one's path early on. By returning to the right course after only a slight detour, one avoids establishing negative patterns or habits. This prompt correction leads to significant good fortune, as it prevents small errors from compounding into larger problems, ensuring that one's journey continues with integrity and towards beneficial outcomes.

### Six in Second
Quiet return. Good fortune.

Choosing to return to a path of righteousness or balance demands both humility and a mastery over one's ego. This line suggests that by aligning oneself with individuals of good character and following their lead, one can return to a better state or situation. This quiet, unassuming return, influenced by the company one keeps, leads to good fortune, as it fosters personal growth, moral integrity, and a supportive environment for positive change.

### Six in Third
Repeated return. Danger. No blame.

This line describes a scenario where one struggles to maintain a permanent return to a positive or beneficial path due to internal conflicts or desires. There's a pattern of moving back to good intentions or behaviors only after succumbing to one's less controlled impulses. This back-and-forth presents a danger, as it might lead to instability or harm from the repeated indulgence in negative habits. However, because there's an effort to return, even if repeatedly, there's no great fault attached to the individual, acknowledging the human struggle between desire and virtue.

### Six in Fourth
Walking in the midst of others, one returns alone.

In a situation where one is surrounded by individuals who are perhaps lost in their own darkness or negative behaviors, this line suggests the importance of finding an inner compass or connection to guide one back to the light or one's true path. The return here is solitary, emphasizing personal responsibility and the strength to navigate back to one's values or goals independently, even when the external environment is not supportive or is leading in the opposite direction.

### Six in Fifth
Noble-hearted return. No remorse.

Returning with the intention of correcting wrongs or aligning with what is just and good is an act of nobility. In this case, one will find no regret in their decision to return. This line highlights that when the motivation for return is rooted in a desire to set things right, whether it's mending relationships, correcting past mistakes, or realigning with one's principles, the act of coming back is free from guilt or sorrow, affirming the moral integrity of the return.

### Six in Sixth
Missing the return. Misfortune. Misfortune from within and without. If armies are set marching in this way, one will in the end suffer a great defeat, disastrous for the ruler of the country. For ten years, it will not be possible to attack again.

This line warns of the peril of failing to recognize or act upon the opportunity to return to a positive path or state due to an obstinate or misguided attitude towards the world. Such a failure leads to misfortune both internally, through personal dissatisfaction or moral decay, and externally, through the consequences of one's actions or decisions. Pursuing actions or policies under these conditions, metaphorically likened to leading an army into battle, will result in significant defeat, bringing disaster not just to the individual but also to the broader community or nation they lead. The mention of a ten-year wait before another "attack" can be mounted suggests a long period of recovery or reflection is necessary, emphasizing the severity of the consequences of missing the chance for return.
