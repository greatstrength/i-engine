# Hexagram 53 - Gradual Progress (Chien)

## Overview

### Summary
When laying foundations that stand the test of time, one must grow with patience and act only with intention at their proper time. The hexagram, with Wind over Mountain, embodies the slow, deliberate ascent toward lasting achievement, where each step is taken with care and purpose to build enduring influence and virtue.

### Judgement
Development. The maiden is given in marriage. Good fortune. Perseverance furthers.

The process by which a maiden is married into a family is a lengthy one. It requires that both the betrothed and their family be well matched and that all necessary formality be carried through in the establishment of the union. This process is a very slow and gradual process, in which the minor actions of all parties serve to grow the relationship over time.

### The Image
On the mountain, a tree: the image of Development. Thus the superior man abides in dignity and virtue in order to improve the mores.

The image of Development portrays a tree growing atop the summit of a mountain. Living in such a harsh environment greatly restricts forward movement, requiring that every step and the consequences be of prime consideration in all regards of growth. In this way, one who effectively embodies this image so works patiently to acquire influence over time through small and persistent acts of kindness and virtue. By gradually strengthening themselves in this way through conscious effort, their influence can naturally extend more readily to those around them.

### Secondary Names
- Development

### Key Terms
- **Gradual Progress**:
  - Advance by degrees, penetrate slowly and surely
  - Infiltrate, adapt
  - Flexible, supple, submissive
  - Permeate, as water
  - Influence, affect
  - Water slowly cutting through an object

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
The wild goose gradually draws to the shore. The young son is in danger. There is talk. No blame.

One begins on their journey to prominence. Yet in the beginning, they are pushed into stillness due to difficulties and adversaries that await them. Such forced stillness gives one the opportunity to better understand the nature of the times and plan accordingly.

### Six in Second
The wild goose gradually draws near the cliff. Eating and drinking in peace and concord. Good fortune.

One has overcome their initially insecure situation and has found the means to live by. Even having gained such a security, they are still of the mind to share it with others, serving to further benefit them.

### Nine in Third
The wild goose gradually draws near the plateau. The man goes forth and does not return. The woman carries a child but does not bring it forth. Misfortune. It furthers one to fight robbers.

When one seeks to develop beyond their station, they find themselves isolated and exposed to attack. Thus, that which one seeks to achieve does not come to fruition while also bringing potential harm. Thus, one ought to abide with the nature of the times and defend themselves from truly unjustified attacks.

### Six in Fourth
The wild goose gradually draws near the tree. Perhaps it will find a flat branch. No blame.

One finds themselves in a dangerous situation as part of their natural development. Yet despite the instability of the situation, one may find a suitable foothold should they remain attentive to their surroundings without bringing further repercussion to themselves.

### Nine in Fifth
The wild goose gradually draws near the summit. For three years the woman has no child. In the end nothing can hinder her. Good fortune.

One isolates themselves from the world for the sake of development. In such a situation, the lack of apparent progress leads their community to distrust them. Yet in due time, the fruits of development shall ripen and all shall serve to benefit upon the return from the summit.

### Nine in Sixth
The wild goose gradually draws near the cloud heights. Its feathers can be used for the sacred dance. Good fortune.

One approaches the end of their development. The echoes of their influence and deeds are like feathers that descend to Earth during the ascent. Such parting wisdom only serves to benefit all.
