# Hexagram 22 - Grace (Pi)

## Overview

### Summary
Grace, or Pi, captures the glow of inner fire rising to crown a steady mountain—a spark of clarity adorning what endures. It speaks to beauty woven into form, where simplicity lifts the everyday into something lasting. One may see this in the quiet rhythm of small tasks handled well, or in the way lasting endeavors—be they personal or grand—thrive when refined with care. Grace isn’t mere sheen; it flows from intent, not trappings, and shines brightest when what’s true within meets what’s shown without.

In daily rounds or broader aims, one might find strength in paring down—making the mundane smooth and the complex clear. This draws others in, not for flash, but for the pull of what’s real and well-crafted. Yet grace has its bounds: it sways amid peers, steadies in community, and rises to higher purpose, but falters if stretched too thin or chased for show. When one holds it steady, even meager means can bloom into good, scorned at first but honored in time. At its peak, grace needs no extra layer—simple and whole, it stands as its own truth, a beauty that endures through the merit of its making.

### Judgement
Grace has success in small matters. It is favorable to undertake something.

When one weaves simplicity into their days—paring down the tangle of little tasks—they may move through them with ease, almost as if in step with a quiet rhythm. Mastery over these small concerns builds a steady hand. With such skill at one’s call, one might turn it toward a larger aim, letting grace guide their efforts into something greater.

### The Image
Fire at the foot of the mountain: the image of Grace. Thus does the superior man proceed when clearing up current affairs. But he dare not decide controversial issues in this way.

Grace glows in its ease—simple, practical, and nimble—fitting well the small tasks and daily tangles one meets. One may lean into this lightness to smooth the clutter of routine, finding strength in its quiet flow. Yet when weighty matters loom—choices that ripple far beyond the day’s edge—grace’s gentle bend falls short. For these, one might seek a steadier gaze, a firm and thoughtful hold, where adaptability alone won’t suffice.

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
He lends grace to his toes, leaves the carriage, and walks.

At the outset, one may stand at a humble spot, eyeing a quicker climb through tempting yet shaky offers. Yet one might turn from the ride, choosing instead to tread the path on foot. This steady pace mirrors grace not just in look but in deed—favoring the quiet worth of effort over gains that stray from honest merit. One could see here that real grace grows from facing the grind with care, step by step, rather than leaning on uncertain props.

### Six in Second
Lends grace to the beard on his chin.

Grace shines truest when it springs from what’s real beneath—depth and meaning woven tight. Yet one may linger on the surface, polishing the outer look—like a beard trimmed for show—while the core stays rough. This line hints at the trap: dressing up what’s hollow risks turning grace into a thin mask. One might ponder if the gleam reflects substance or just a fleeting sheen, for without roots, it fades to mere vanity.

### Nine in Third
Graceful and moist. Constant perseverance brings good fortune.

When one moves among peers with grace, a shared rhythm takes hold—tasks align, and the joy of it hums through all. This harmony brews a heady thrill, flowing smooth like wine, warm and rich. Yet one may feel its pull too keenly; among kin or close ones, losing step could spill into stumbles, inviting their jeer or dismay. If one sways too far into the glow, it might dim under scrutiny. One could keep a steady pulse instead, savoring the tide without drowning in it. Through such balance, grace holds firm and yields good.

### Six in Fourth
Grace or simplicity? A white horse comes as if on wings. He is not a robber, he will woo at the right time.

Within the communal sphere, one may consider how to extend their grace—opting for bold display to capture attention, or refining a restrained approach with patience and precision. The choice resonates among peers: flair may impress, yet simplicity proves lasting. One might favor the latter, steady as a white horse in motion—pure, swift, and capable. With such skill honed, one could attract genuine collaborators from the community, not opportunists seeking gain, but colleagues who align at the right time, prepared to advance a shared aim.

### Six in Fifth
Grace in hills and gardens. The roll of silk is meager and small. Humiliation, but in the end good fortune.

One may drift from the lowland’s grind—where status and wealth reign—and climb toward the spare grace of hills and gardens. The roll of silk, a thin offering, marks their entry—perhaps light on skill or means, a humble thread that draws sidelong looks or scorn from below. Yet grace rests not in what one holds, but in what one intends. With little polish but ample drive, one might seek simplicity’s truth, and there it lies, even amid the sting of doubt. Those who see past the surface—the real ones—may spot this spark and lend their strength. From such roots, good fortune grows, a higher gain beyond the weight of things.

### Nine in Sixth
Simple grace. No blame.

Here, grace stands bare—its truest form, where what one shows flows clear from what one is. No polish or flourish need apply; simplicity itself shines as the sage’s mark. One may carry their words, deeds, and presence so true that extras fall away, leaving only what’s real. In this quiet alignment, fault finds no hold—a stillness unfolds, deep and whole, revealing grace as the heart’s own truth.
