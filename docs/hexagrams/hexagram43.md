# Hexagram 43 - Breakthrough (Kuai)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Lake above Heaven, corresponds to the third month of the year, from April to May, a time when the power of darkness is waning, and only resolute action can bring about transformative change. This period calls for decisive effort to overcome lingering obstacles, harnessing the rising light to shift conditions toward renewal and progress.

### Judgement
Break-through. One must resolutely make the matter known at the court of the king. It must be announced truthfully. Danger. It is necessary to notify one's own city. It does not further to resort to arms. It furthers to undertake something.

Breakthrough discusses the nature of subjugating evil. While such a fight does require resoluteness and endurance of character, it ought not be carried out directly, as the darkness shall continue feeding off the light. Instead, one ought to defend themselves against that which they bring to light and focus on energetically striving for the good.

### The Image
The lake has risen up to heaven: the image of Breakthrough. Thus the superior man dispenses riches downward and refrains from resting on his virtue.

One of enduring character understands that, like a cloudburst over a lake, one ought not be stuck in obstinacy until conflict arises but instead remain impressionable to the advice and feedback from others.

### Secondary Names
- Resoluteness
- Deciding

### Key Terms
- **Decide**:
  - Choose
  - Resolute, prompt, decisive, certain, clear
  - Separate, fork, cut off, part, divide in two

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Mighty in the forward-striding toes. When one goes and is not equal to the task, one makes a mistake.

A leader shall not advance in the beginning, understanding that the darkness is still too strong. Should one seek to advance regardless, they shall be mired with error.

### Nine in Second
A cry of alarm. Arms at evening and at night. Fear nothing.

A leader of enduring character is on guard from what is not yet within sight and on alert for what is not yet within hearing. In this way, they shall fear no danger.

### Nine in Third
To be powerful in the cheekbones brings misfortune. The superior man is firmly resolved. He walks alone and is caught in the rain. He is bespattered, and people murmur against him. No blame.

A leader is in a relationship with followers that do not suit them. Yet when the time for resolute action comes, the leader is not in a position to end the relationship amicably. In this way, rumors are spread about their suspect, if not traitorous, character, but no ill repercussions stem from this.

### Nine in Fourth
There is no skin on his thighs, and walking comes hard. If a man were to let himself be led like a sheep, remorse would disappear. But if these words are heard, they will not be believed.

A leader who is inwardly insecure yet outwardly obstinate shall always encounter failure, as sound words of advice will never be believed by them.

### Nine in Fifth
In dealing with weeds, firm resolution is necessary. Walking in the middle remains free of blame.

Followers in a position of leadership, like weeds, are difficult to remove. Working with these sorts of people requires firm resoluteness, but the matter must be handled in the correct way to avoid ill repercussions stemming from action.

### Six in Sixth
No cry. In the end misfortune comes.

It is important that a leader, when rooting out evil, do so thoroughly, for allowing even a fragment of it to escape shall lead to misfortune down the line.
