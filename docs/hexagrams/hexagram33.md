# Hexagram 33 - Retreat (Tun)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram corresponds to the sixth month, from July to August, a period where the influence of winter begins to emerge. Here, the power of darkness grows, prompting the light to retreat, ensuring it's not overtaken. This increase in darkness is a natural progression of the season, but the wise choose retreat as a strategic response.

### Judgement
Retreat. Success. In what is small, perseverance furthers.

The situation described here is one where a stronger, less benevolent force is advancing, making retreat the prudent course of action. However, this retreat isn't about running away in panic but about tactical withdrawal to gain small, strategic victories by delaying or thwarting the enemy's advance.

### The Image
Mountain under heaven: the image of Retreat. Thus the superior man keeps the inferior man at a distance, not angrily but with reserve.

The imagery of Heaven above the Mountain illustrates a scenario where one faces an adversary. It signifies having an inner reserve of strength or wisdom that allows one to maintain distance from negative influences or hostile forces without confrontation, embodying a calm, reserved approach rather than an aggressive one.

### Secondary Names
- Retiring

### Key Terms
- **Retreat**:
  - Retire, withdraw, run away, escape, flee, hide yourself
  - Disappear, withdraw into obscurity, become invisible
  - Secluded, anti-social
  - Fool or trick someone
  - Satisfaction from withdrawing

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
At the tail in retreat. This is dangerous. One must not wish to undertake anything.

In a retreat, being at the tail of the formation is akin to being at the most vulnerable position, directly exposed to enemy advances. The advice here is clear: do not engage in any new endeavors or actions. The safest and most prudent approach is to remain still, avoiding any initiative that might attract attention or lead to further peril. This line underscores the importance of caution and strategic withdrawal, recognizing when the situation calls for inaction rather than action.

### Six in Second
He holds him fast with yellow oxhide. No one can tear him loose.

This line signifies a situation where one is committed to a moral or ethical stance, symbolized by the firm grip of "yellow oxhide." When someone is resolute in their dedication to doing what is right, no adversary or challenge can sway or dislodge them from their path. It highlights the strength and resilience derived from integrity and determination, suggesting that in retreat, one's commitment to principle can be a form of steadfastness and defense against those who would oppose or corrupt it.

### Nine in Third
A halted retreat is nerve-wracking and dangerous. To retain people as men- and maidservants brings good fortune.

In situations where retreat is momentarily stopped, which can be fraught with tension and peril, making use of the resources or support one has is crucial. Even if the resources or the support available seem limited, leveraging them effectively can lead to good fortune. This line suggests that during retreat, one should not overlook the value of the people around them, recognizing that maintaining or utilizing the loyalty and capabilities of those close, even in service roles, can be a significant asset, helping to navigate through or mitigate the dangers of a paused retreat.

### Nine in Fourth
Voluntary retreat brings good fortune to the superior man and downfall to the inferior man.

When someone of high moral or intellectual standing chooses to withdraw from negative or corrupt influences, they benefit greatly from this disassociation. This voluntary retreat allows them to preserve their integrity and gain freedom from harmful environments or relationships. Conversely, those of lesser character or intent, who depend on or benefit from the presence of the superior individual, find themselves at a disadvantage or facing decline when left to their own devices. This line emphasizes the wisdom of knowing when to step back for personal growth and the natural consequences that follow for those who do not share the same virtues.

### Nine in Fifth
Friendly retreat. Perseverance brings good fortune.

When one decides to retreat with a firm resolve but in a manner that maintains cordiality and respect, they can disengage from the current circumstances without creating enmity. This approach not only preserves relationships but also sets the stage for good fortune through perseverance. It suggests that even in withdrawal, one can act with dignity and goodwill, ensuring that the retreat is seen not as a defeat but as a strategic move that benefits all parties in the long run.

### Nine in Sixth
Cheerful retreat. Everything serves to further.

This line describes a retreat that embodies the wisdom of a sage, in such harmony with the natural flow of events or the needs of the moment that it's undertaken with a sense of cheerfulness or optimism. When one's withdrawal is perfectly aligned with the circumstances, every aspect of this retreat contributes to progress or beneficial outcomes. It signifies that the act of retreating, when done at the right time and with the right attitude, can be a positive, forward-moving step, enhancing one's position or well-being rather than diminishing it.
