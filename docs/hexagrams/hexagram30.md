# Hexagram 30 - The Clinging (Li)

## Overview

### Summary

This hexagram is composed of the trigram of Fire repeated, where a yielding line is sandwiched between two firm ones, suggesting a dependency or clinging relationship. This configuration reflects how fire, lacking its own form, clings to a source of fuel to burn and give light. If we consider the inverse of this hexagram to symbolize the nature of the human soul, then Fire represents the outward manifestation or expression of that soul.

### Judgement

The Clinging. Perseverance furthers. It brings success. Care of the cow brings good fortune.

Everything that emits light must adhere to a source of sustenance. Just as plants cling to the earth and humans to the light of self-awareness, society values and elevates those who act with righteousness, symbolized by "the care of the cow." This docility or adherence to what is right in man can only manifest when there's clarity about one's own limitations and how best to utilize them in service to others or the greater good.

### The Image

That which is bright rises twice: the image of Fire. Thus the great man, by perpetuating this brightness, illuminates the four quarters of the world.

When clarity is maintained and perpetuated, it has the power to spread far and fast, much like fire or light, influencing and enlightening a broad expanse.

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First

The footprints run crisscross. If one is seriously intent, no blame.

Upon first gaining awareness, like waking up in the morning when thoughts might be scattered or confused ("footprints run crisscross"), this line suggests a moment of disorientation or initial confusion. However, if one holds a firm and serious intention towards their goal, they can navigate through this disarray without fault. Seriousness of purpose provides the clarity and direction needed to rise above the initial chaos of thought or action, leading to successful outcomes without blame.

### Six in Second

Yellow light. Supreme good fortune.

Yellow, often associated with the mean or balance, here symbolizes the "golden" or highest quality of light, representing clarity, wisdom, and enlightenment. Acting with or upon such clarity, which is of great purity and understanding, leads to outcomes of the highest fortune. This line emphasizes that when one's actions are guided by this balanced, clear light, the results will be exceptionally beneficial, reflecting the supreme good that comes from aligning with one's true path or purpose.

### Nine in Third

In the light of the setting sun, men either beat the pot and sing or loudly bewail the approach of old age. Misfortune.

This line reflects on how individuals spend their later years or moments of life's sunset. Some might live recklessly or in celebration, "beating the pot and singing," under the false assumption that there's always more time ahead. However, when the reality of aging or the end of life's day sets in, they may find themselves lamenting their lost opportunities or the physical diminishment that comes with age. Such a squandering of the clarity and time one has been given leads to misfortune, as it represents a failure to use one's awareness and time wisely, resulting in regrets when it's too late to make amends.

### Nine in Fourth

Its coming is sudden; it flames up, dies down, is thrown away.

This line describes the nature of a fire or an endeavor that flares brightly but lacks the sustenance to last. Without adequate fuel or support, such a venture will quickly burn out and be discarded, leaving little to no lasting impact. It serves as a metaphor for actions or projects undertaken without proper foundation or resources, warning that enthusiasm or initial brilliance alone is not enough to achieve enduring results; they must be backed by substance and persistence.

### Six in Fifth

Tears in floods, sighing and lamenting. Good fortune.

This line speaks to the profound emotional response one might have when clarity or enlightenment leads to a significant change in one's perspective or life direction. The grief, expressed through "tears in floods" and lamentations, is a natural and necessary part of letting go of past illusions or attachments. Despite the pain or sorrow involved, this emotional release is considered good fortune because it clears the way for new understanding, growth, and opportunities, signifying a transformative moment where one's heart and mind are purified for future endeavors.

### Nine in Sixth

The king uses it to march forth and chastise. Then it’s best to kill the leaders and take captive the followers. No blame.

This line metaphorically addresses how to deal with negative or harmful habits or behaviors. The "king" represents the aspect of oneself or leadership that takes decisive action. By targeting and eliminating the "leaders" — the core or root causes of the problematic behavior — while capturing or redirecting the "followers" — the less harmful or influenced behaviors — one can effectively reform or correct the situation without incurring blame. This approach ensures that only the most detrimental elements are eradicated, allowing for the reformation or reintegration of the less harmful aspects, thereby fostering positive change without unnecessary harm.
