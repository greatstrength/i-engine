# Hexagram 2 - The Receptive (K'un)

## Overview

### Summary
The Receptive, or K’un, embodies the quiet power of yin, a force of support, devotion, and adaptability that complements the bold energy of The Creative. Like the earth itself, it fosters growth without forcing it, offering a steady presence where all things find their pace. In human bonds—whether spouse to partner, child to parent, or aide to leader—it excels not by leading but by nurturing and sustaining what others set in motion. This is no equal partnership but a vital harmony, thriving on devoted support. When this balance tips—through overreach or neglect—tension clouds the connection.

For one embracing The Receptive, success lies in yielding wisely, blending strength with flexibility to meet life’s demands. It calls for restraint over ambition, a readiness to serve without seeking the spotlight. In this way, one builds trust and stability, fostering outcomes that benefit all. To push against this role invites conflict; to align with it ensures enduring fortune.

### Judgement
The Receptive brings about sublime success, furthering through the presence of the mare. If the superior man undertakes something and tries to lead, he goes astray. But, if he follows, he might find guidance. It is favorable to find friends in the west and south, to forego friends in the east and north. Quiet perseverance brings good fortune.

The Receptive ensures sublime success when one blends strength to persevere with docility to adapt, a situational awareness embodied by the mare. One who seeks to lead with strength when they ought to follow shall only be misguided in their leadership. Only by applying strength gently and intently, as the moment requires, does one gain true direction. In such times, it serves one well to find friends in the south, where activity thrives, and to collect wisdom in the west, where experience deepens. It is not the time to conclude tasks, as the north implies, nor to start anew, as the east beckons.

### The Image
The earth's condition is receptive devotion. Thus the superior man who has breadth of character carries the outer world.

To reflect The Receptive, one must offer steady support to all around them, embracing both the virtuous and the flawed. The earth bears all without judgment—its vastness nurtures life in every form. So too must one cultivate a character wide enough to endure and uplift, wielding devotion as a quiet strength. Through this, one carries the world’s weight with grace.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Where there is hoarfrost underfoot, solid ice is not far off.

The first traces of stillness emerge, as hoarfrost signals winter’s approach. This marks a shift from action to quiet, a time when one’s surroundings grow still and call for yielding. To heed this, one must turn inward, adapting with care to the present. Perseverance begins here, in meeting the moment as it is.

### Six in Second
Straight, square, great. Without purpose, yet nothing remains unfurthered.

This line reveals the quiet power of one’s inner qualities—steadfast, balanced, and vast. Though the time may not call for action, these traits stand ready, ensuring nothing falls short when need arises. By holding firm without forcing intent, one furthers all things naturally, effort preserved for what endures.

### Six in Third
One is able to remain persevering. If by chance they are in service to a king, seek not works, but bring to completion.

This line counsels quiet persistence to sidestep strife with those nearby. By staying devoted and unseen, one preserves harmony. When called to serve, direct all effort toward completing the task at hand, not chasing recognition or reward. True strength lies in fulfillment, not fanfare.

### Six in Fourth
A tied-up sack. No blame, no praise.

Should one chase prominence in the community through their own rise, they invite envy and strife from others. By staying devoted to their work, like a tied-up sack, they draw neither blame nor praise from those around them, securing peace through restraint.

### Six in Fifth
A yellow lower garment brings supreme, good fortune.

Here, one serves the world through steady devotion, marked by the yellow lower garment—Earth’s humble hue. Acting with true intent, they reveal their nature through quiet work, not bold display. This sincerity fosters outcomes that lift all, securing lasting favor.

### Six in Sixth
Dragons fight in the meadow. Their blood is black and yellow.

When one strives to lead instead of yielding, they provoke those above, igniting fierce strife. The black and yellow dragons—symbols of Heaven’s might and Earth’s depth—collide, wounding each other in the fray. This warns of the harm that comes from rejecting one’s place. To rise against the moment’s call courts ruin.
