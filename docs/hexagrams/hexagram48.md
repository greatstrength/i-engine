# Hexagram 48 - The Well (Ching)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Water over Wood, conveys the idea of an inexhaustible source of nourishment and energy. In the same way that one draws water from the well and plants draw water from the ground through their roots, so too do leaders draw energies from their inexhaustible well of time to carry out undertakings. It symbolizes the heart of a community, a constant resource that sustains through change, provided it is maintained and accessed with care.

### Judgement
The Well. The town may have changed, but the well cannot be changed. It neither decreases nor increases. They come and go and draw from the well. If one gets down almost to the water and the rope does not go all the way, or the jug breaks, it brings misfortune.

The well represents the heart of the community. While the town may change as people come and go, the well remains, allowing the people to draw water from it. Should the means be lost to draw the water, ill outcomes are sure to follow.

### The Image
Water over wood: the image of The Well. Thus the superior man encourages the people at their work, and exhorts them to help one another.

The fibers of a plant work in harmony to draw water upward for the sake of nourishment. So too must a leader create harmony amongst their followers such that they may all work towards the common undertaking more effectively.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
One does not drink the mud of the well. No animals come to an old well.

A leader who throws themselves away is no longer bothered by or sought after by anyone. Like a neglected well filled with mud, their potential remains untapped, and they become irrelevant to the community. This line warns of the consequences of abandoning one’s purpose or failing to maintain one’s inner resources, leading to isolation and uselessness.

### Nine in Second
At the well hole one shoots fishes. The jug is broken and leaks.

A leader has good qualities but neglects to use them. They associate with the wrong followers and are unable to undertake anything. The imagery of shooting fish in a well and a broken jug suggests squandered potential and ineffective efforts, highlighting the need to align with suitable allies and maintain the means to access one’s inner resources effectively.

### Nine in Third
The well is cleaned, but no one drinks from it. This is my heart's sorrow, for one might draw from it. If the king were clear-minded, good fortune might be enjoyed in common.

A leader possesses the qualities to carry out an undertaking, but there are no kings of clear mind willing to risk working with them. This comes with the sorrow of the followers and those who serve to be helped by the leader. The cleaned well, ready but unused, reflects unfulfilled potential due to lack of recognition or trust from those in power, causing collective regret.

### Six in Fourth
The well is being lined. No blame.

A leader must make themselves unavailable during times of personal growth, as represented by the lining of the well. While they may be of no help to others, their retreat for the sake of development bears no ill repercussions. This line underscores the importance of self-care and preparation, as strengthening one’s foundation is a blameless act that ensures future readiness.

### Nine in Fifth
In the well there is a clear, cold spring. From which one can drink.

A leader possesses all of the appropriate qualities, serving their followers and the needs of the king alike. The clear, cold spring symbolizes a well-maintained source of nourishment and wisdom, accessible and beneficial to all. This line highlights the leader’s ability to fulfill their role effectively, providing sustenance and guidance to the community.

### Six in Sixth
One draws from the well without hindrance. It is dependable. Supreme good fortune.

A sage draws from an inexhaustible source of knowledge and wisdom that serves to bring great outcomes to those who are blessed to receive them. The dependable well, freely accessible, represents a leader or sage whose wisdom flows unhindered, benefiting all and yielding supreme good fortune through their enduring service to the community.
