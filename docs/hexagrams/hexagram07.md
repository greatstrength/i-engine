# Hexagram 7 - The Army (Shih)

## Overview

### Summary
In times of the army, Water within Earth channels danger through the masses, strength drawn from disciplined flow. One marshals order and perseverance, led by a seasoned hand—first in strict array, then amid ranks with care, wary of greed’s loot or rash retreat, seizing foes near, until victory crowns the worthy with fiefs. Misfortune stalks the undisciplined, but steadfast command yields good fortune, forging a state from chaos through trust and merit.

### Judgement
The Army. The army needs perseverance and a strong man. Good fortune without blame.

In times of the army, one rallies the masses with steadfast order, needing perseverance and a strong leader. A seasoned general’s resolve ensures discipline, yielding good fortune without blame. Strength and structure turn chaos to triumph.

### The Image
In the middle of the earth is water: the image of The Army. Thus the superior man increases his masses by generosity towards the people.

In times of the army, Water within Earth flows as the people’s strength, sustaining the whole. One ought to nurture the masses with generosity, easing burdens and upholding justice. This bonds them to the cause, swelling ranks with loyal resolve.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
The army must set forth in proper order. If the order is not good, misfortune threatens.

In times of the army, one marshals forces in strict order, grounding strength in discipline. Without this, chaos looms, threatening misfortune. Proper array ensures readiness, staving off disarray’s doom.

### Nine in Second
In the midst of the army. Good fortune. No blame. The King bestows a triple decoration.

In times of the army, one stands amid the ranks, leading with care and unity. Soldiers repay this bond with strength, securing good fortune without blame. The king’s triple honor crowns such steadfast command.

### Six in Third
Perchance the army carries corpses in the wagon. Misfortune.

In times of the army, one finds wagons laden with corpses, spoils of greed over need. This lapse in discipline clogs the march, courting misfortune. Victory falters when gain overshadows purpose.

### Six in Fourth
The army retreats. No blame.

In times of the army, one withdraws the ranks before overwhelming might, free of blame. This retreat, a measured step, preserves strength for later gain. Wisdom favors patience over rash defeat.

### Six in Fifth
There is game in the field. It furthers one to catch it. Without blame. Let the eldest lead the army. The younger transports corpses; then perseverance brings misfortune.

In times of the army, one sees game in the field, flushed by the enemy’s advance through the woods. Striking swiftly brings no blame, guided by the eldest’s seasoned hand. Yet if the younger haul loot, greed’s persistence courts misfortune’s sting.

### Six in Sixth
The great prince issues commands, founds states, vests families with fiefs. Inferior people should not be employed.

In times of the army, one, as great prince, decrees order, founding states and fiefdoms for the worthy. Merit earns enduring rank, while inferior folk gain naught but fleeting coin. Victory’s close shuns the base, cementing rule through trust.
