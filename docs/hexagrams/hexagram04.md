# Hexagram 4 - Youthful Folly (Mêng)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of Youthful Folly captures the essence of the fool, an individual yet to gain the wisdom of experience. Like one who gazes over a cliff at the treacherous sea below, this person fails to recognize the peril due to their inexperience, leading to actions that overlook personal safety.

Being a fool isn't inherently blameworthy, provided one acknowledges their novice status and respects their need for guidance. Here, the importance of seeking mentorship shines through, with humility and openness being key to true learning.

### Judgement
Youthful Folly has success. It is not I who seek the young fool; the young fool seeks me. At the first oracle I inform him. If he asks two or three times, it is importunity. If he importunes, I give him no information. Perseverance furthers.

A mentor, rich in experience, should guide the student by tailoring instructions to match their nature and readiness, ensuring neither an excess nor a deficit of information. This mentor is firm in teaching methods, setting clear boundaries and demanding decorum. If the student shows a lack of receptivity, the mentor must pause instruction, waiting for the learner to realign their attitude, possibly through introspection or consequence.

### The Image
A spring wells up at the foot of the mountain: the image of Youthful Folly. Thus the superior man fosters his character by thoroughness in all that he does.

Much like how a mountain spring flows to fill the hollows, avoiding stagnation, those in the folly of youth must slowly accumulate knowledge through persistent practice and a positive, intentional approach to learning, reflecting the natural flow of growth and the cycles of change inherent in I Ching's teachings.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
To make a fool develop, it furthers one to apply discipline. The fetters should be removed.

When seeking to acquire knowledge, the proper mentor shall instill discipline upon their student to ensure a steadfast seriousness towards their work. It is important for the mentor to instill such a behavior of seriousness be cultivated in a space that promotes free thinking and adaptability without devolving into mindless drill and ritualism.

### Nine in Second
To bear with fools in kindliness brings good fortune. To know how to take women brings good fortune. The son is capable of taking charge of the household.

In the quest for knowledge, a mentor must instill discipline to cultivate a sincere commitment to learning in their student. This discipline should foster an environment where free thought and adaptability thrive, ensuring that the learning process does not degenerate into mere routine or rote learning. The essence of this line is to balance structure with freedom, allowing the student to grow without the constraints of excessive control, much like removing fetters to enable true progress.

### Six in Third
Take not a maiden who, when she sees a man of bronze, loses possession of herself. Nothing furthers.

A student should not squander their potential on fleeting or shallow pursuits and the allure of easy success. True achievement comes from within, through persistent effort and a steadfast resolve. By focusing on their unique inner strengths, one can cultivate genuine success, rather than being distracted by external appearances or momentary fascinations that ultimately lead nowhere.

### Six in Fourth
Entangled folly brings humiliation.

When a student becomes mired in stubbornness or distracted by fanciful notions, disconnecting from the practical aspects of learning, a wise mentor must know when to withdraw. Allowing the student to face the natural consequences of their actions serves as a form of judgment, teaching through experience. Only when the student shows genuine repentance and a renewed openness to learning can the mentor resume their guidance, ensuring that the lesson of humility and the importance of grounding oneself in reality has been learned.

### Six in Fifth
Child-like folly brings good fortune.

Embracing learning with the innocence and openness of a child, free from arrogance, is the path to genuine growth. By letting go of pride and accepting instruction with humility, one will undoubtedly find the assistance and guidance they need, highlighting the virtue of a teachable spirit in the journey towards wisdom and success.

### Nine in Sixth
In punishing folly, it does not further one to commit transgressions. The only thing that furthers is to prevent transgressions.

When dealing with a student who has become incorrigible, correction is necessary. However, any form of punishment must be just and aimed at the restoration of learning receptivity, not as an act of vengeance or excess. The true purpose here is to guide the student back to the path of learning, preventing further missteps by fostering an environment where discipline leads to growth rather than further transgressions.
