# Hexagram 49 - Revolution (Ko)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of Revolution, with Lake over Fire, points to the ever-present notion of change in nature, akin to a molting or shedding of old skin for a new one. Just as people naturally outgrow interests that no longer serve them, so too do regimes of the State rise and fall with the passage of time and the will of the people. It signifies a transformative shift, where opposing forces resolve into a new order through deliberate, principled action.

### Judgement
Revolution. On your own day you are believed. Supreme success, furthering through perseverance, remorse disappears.

The trigrams of the Lake over Fire show two forces of opposing nature in a position to openly combat each other. While Opposition shows the Fire rising above the Lake to highlight their individual wills, the Lake in this case falls back towards the Fire, extinguishing it and creating a new set of conditions from which to build. The natural seasons of the year operate in this way, each season representing a given presence of light and darkness and their given positions. In this way, one of enduring character sees these natural conflicts as inherent in nature and names them such for the good of the People to follow.

### The Image
Fire in the lake: the image of Revolution. Thus the superior man sets the calendar in order and makes the seasons clear.

Changes in the established order are complex, especially so should they come to Revolution. As a grave matter, a leader must be called not to acquire personal advantage but to set what was once stagnant back into motion in accordance with the time. Should a leader act in this way, they shall gain the support of their followers, removing any cause for regret.

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Wrapped in the hide of a yellow cow.

Changes in the established order ought only be implemented through Revolution only if all other means of reform have been exhausted. In this way, a leader called to revolution must exercise correct restraint, as represented by the "hide of a yellow cow," and wait for the appropriate time to act.

### Six in Second
When one's own day comes, one may create revolution. Starting brings good fortune.

Should a leader with the needed capabilities be called to lead a Revolution, starting serves to benefit them when the correct time of action has come.

### Nine in Third
Starting brings misfortune. Perseverance brings danger. When talk of revolution has gone rounds three times, one may commit himself, and men will believe him.

A leader wishes to carry out a change through Revolution, however rushing in solely on the idea of change serves to benefit no one. In fact, it may make the position of the leader untenable and open them to attack. Only when a change is worthy of Revolution and there is verified support from the people, or it has "gone rounds three times," can a leader commit themselves and find the support they need.

### Nine in Fourth
Remorse disappears. Men believe him. Changing the form of government brings good fortune.

When a leader is called to change the form of government through Revolution, their actions remove all reason for regret and win over the hearts of the people. This serves to further everyone.

### Nine in Fifth
The great man changes like a tiger. Even before he questions the oracle, he is believed.

The tiger is a symbol of both strength and political authority. When the call for a Revolution is felt by the People, writ large, a leader that both embodies the principles of the Revolution and is capable of carrying it out shall not be opposed in their victory. Oftentimes, tenuous situations like this require consultation from an oracle, but as the leader is believed "before he questions the oracle," there is a connotation of inevitable success.

### Six in Sixth
The superior man changes like a panther. The inferior man molts in the face. Starting brings misfortune. To remain persevering brings good fortune.

A leader has carried out a revolution and has established themselves as the new authority. With this change in power, they have acquired new followers. However, these new followers embody the spirit of the Revolution superficially. Should the leader persevere in embodying the Revolutionary spirit as before, the new followers shall come around in time.
