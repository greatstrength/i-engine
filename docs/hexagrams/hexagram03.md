# Hexagram 3 - Difficulty at the Beginning (Chun)

## Overview

### Summary
When Heaven and Earth first converge to bring forth creation, their initial interaction is fraught with challenges, marked by a chaotic abundance. The external environment is wild and perilous, while internally, energy abounds without clear direction or purpose. These are the obstacles one must navigate when embarking on new ventures or ideas.

### Judgement
Difficulty at the beginning works supreme success, furthering through perseverance. Nothing should be undertaken. It furthers one to appoint helpers.

Beginning any new endeavor is inherently difficult; the nascent form of what one seeks to create is still battling to solidify into something tangible. Yet, perseverance in this struggle promises eventual success once a concrete form is realized. Here, the guidance of a mentor or teacher becomes invaluable, not only to impart wisdom but also to create an environment where learning can occur. This mentorship is crucial, offering a beacon of light through the initial darkness of confusion.

### The Image
Clouds and thunder: the image of Difficulty at the Beginning. Thus the superior man brings order out of the confusion.

Those of superior character recognize that by steadfastly adhering to what is right, these early difficulties will be surmounted. Through continued learning and diligent effort, one uncovers the solutions to these challenges, transforming initial chaos into order, and potential into achievement.

### Secondary Names
- Sprouting

### Key Terms
- **Sprout**:
  - Begin or cause to grow
  - Assemble, accumulate, amass, hoard
  - Establish a base of operations, establish troops at the borders
  - Difficult, painful, arduous; the difficulties at the beginning of an endeavor

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Hesitation and hindrance. It furthers one to remain persevering. It furthers one to appoint helpers.

When faced with initial difficulties, one should not rush but take time to reflect. However, this pause should not lead to abandonment of one's goals. Instead, seeking assistance from the right individuals is key to advancing properly.

### Six in Second
Difficulties pile up. Horse and wagon part. He is not a robber; he wants to woo when the time comes. The maiden is chaste, she does not pledge herself. Ten years, then she pledges herself.

Help to surmount initial challenges might arrive unexpectedly but with conditions that could prove detrimental as things develop. It's wise to wait until one is genuinely prepared to accept such aid at a more suitable time.

### Six in Third
Whoever hunts a deer without the forester only loses his way in the forest. The superior man understands the signs of the time and prefers to desist. Going on brings humiliation.

Embarking on endeavors without proper guidance leads one astray. One will inevitably reach a point where direction is needed. Premature attempts to tackle problems without assistance invite misfortune.

### Six in Fourth
Horse and wagon part. Strive for union. To go brings good fortune. Everything acts to further.

Necessity drives one to act despite lacking support or means. Assistance will emerge on the path, but one must humbly accept and employ whatever help is offered.

### Nine in Fifth
Difficulties in blessing. A little perseverance brings good fortune. Great perseverance brings misfortune.

Gaining support for new ventures is challenging when their value isn't recognized. One ought to expect more resistance than aid from peers. It's wise to develop privately, waiting for a more receptive setting before advancing.

### Six in Sixth
Horse and wagon part. Bloody tears flow.

Overwhelmed by the initial challenges, one abandons the effort before it can truly commence, marked by deep sorrow and defeat.
