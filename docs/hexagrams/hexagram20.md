# Hexagram 20 - Contemplation (Kuan)

## Overview

### Summary
The superior person, serving as a Divine Medium, looks both up to Heaven and down to Earth, contemplating how the ways of Earth are manifest from Heaven. To be in Contemplation is to have one Eye on the divine and the other on the human, becoming a paragon of moral conduct. This hexagram is associated with the eighth month from September to October, a time in which through great contemplation, the harvest is judged for its value.

### Judgement
Contemplation. The ablution has been made, but not yet the offering. Full of trust they look up to him.

Religious ceremonies were often undertaken to awaken a certain divine understanding of the world in its participants, highlighting the importance of the task and the level of trust exhibited. One who has washed themselves clean, symbolized by the ablution, gains the trust of their people even before the offering is made, reflecting the deep bond and reliance placed in leadership that is both spiritually and practically engaged.

### The Image
The Wind blows over the Earth, the image of Contemplation. Thus the kings of old visited the vast regions of the realm, contemplated the people, and gave them instruction.

In the same way that a king must travel the lands far and wide to see the labors of the people and to provide instruction as to their better use, so too must one of superior character be able to look upon themselves in entirety to provide corrective action to habits considered of hindering nature.

### Secondary Names
- Viewing

### Key Terms
- **Contemplate**:
  - View, look at from a distance or height
  - Examine, judge, conjecture about
  - Divination
  - Idea, point of view
  - Instruct, inform, point out, make known
  - A monastery, an observatory, a tower, a marker

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Boy-like contemplation. For an inferior man, no blame. For a superior man, humiliation.

Observing from afar, with a lack of deep understanding, is akin to a child's view of the world. For those of lesser insight or position, this superficial contemplation carries no fault, as they might still derive some benefit from the situation. However, for someone of superior character or responsibility, such detachment or lack of engagement with the core issues at hand is a source of shame. It underscores the expectation that those with greater wisdom or leadership roles should not remain at a distance but should engage closely with the nuances of reality to truly guide or influence effectively.

### Six in Second
Contemplation through the crack of the door. Furthering for the perseverance of a woman.

This line describes a contemplation that is narrow or restricted, like peering out through a crack in a door. This limited perspective is associated with a more inward or self-focused approach, which is here linked to feminine energy—not in a derogatory sense, but in acknowledging the traditional association with inwardness, patience, or subtlety. Such an approach might not see the full picture but can still be beneficial, particularly in terms of perseverance, suggesting that sometimes a cautious, detailed, or protective contemplation can lead to success, especially in situations where a gentle, persistent approach is needed.

### Six in Third
Contemplation of my life decides the choice between advance and retreat.

This line points to a crucial moment of self-reflection where one examines their own life and the consequences of their actions on others. Through this introspection, one can determine whether their life has moved forward in a positive direction or if there has been a regression. This contemplation is pivotal for making informed decisions about whether to continue advancing on the current path or to retreat and reconsider one's approach, highlighting the importance of self-awareness in personal growth and leadership.

### Six in Fourth
Contemplation of the light of the kingdom. It furthers one to exert influence as the guest of a king.

Possessing wisdom that benefits the state, one finds themselves in a position where they are sought after to serve in an advisory or influential role, akin to an honored guest of the king. This position allows one to exert influence with respect and freedom, not as a mere instrument of state policy but as a valued advisor or sage. This line highlights the power of insightful contemplation, leading to recognition and the opportunity to shape governance or societal direction from a place of honor and autonomy.

### Nine in Fifth
Contemplation of my life. The superior man is without blame.

A leader or person in a high position must continually engage in self-reflection to comprehend how their decisions and actions impact others. By doing so, they ensure their conduct leads to positive outcomes, maintaining integrity and avoiding fault. This line emphasizes that true leadership involves not just outward action but also inward contemplation, leading to moral and effective governance or influence, free from blame due to the conscientiousness of their self-assessment.

### Nine in Sixth
Contemplation of his life. The superior man is without blame.

This concluding line refers to an individual who has transcended personal desires or ego, focusing instead on understanding the universal laws of Heaven and Earth. Through such profound contemplation, they align their life with higher principles, leading to actions or decisions that do not create additional issues or conflicts. This level of introspection and alignment with cosmic order allows them to operate with such wisdom and integrity that they remain blameless, embodying the highest form of leadership or personal conduct where one's actions are in harmony with the natural and moral order.
