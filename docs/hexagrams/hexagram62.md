# Hexagram 62 - Preponderance of the Small (Hsiao Kuo)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of Preponderance of the Small, with Thunder over Mountain, contrasts with Preponderance of the Great by placing yielding lines at the center, surrounded by firm lines, creating a state of inner weakness and outer receptivity. This configuration calls for extreme prudence, as one lacks the strength to exert significant influence. Success lies in attending to small matters with modesty and caution, avoiding grand undertakings that exceed one’s capacity.

### Judgement
Preponderance of the Small. Success. Perseverance furthers. Small things may be done; great things should not be done. The flying bird brings the message: it is not well to strive upward, it is well to remain below. Great good fortune.

Success may be found when persevering in Modesty. In this way, small things ought to be attended to. One is not capable of a great undertaking at this time. It is also prudent to not strive for attention or promotion, as keeping a low profile serves to benefit all.

### The Image
Thunder on the mountain: the image of Preponderance of the Small. Thus in his conduct the superior man gives preponderance to reverence. In bereavement he gives preponderance to grief. In his expenditures he gives preponderance to thrift.

Thunder over the Mountain pictures movement reinforced by firm rigidity. This corresponds to adherence towards one’s purpose. By being conscious of one's duty in this way, they are better able to keep their emotions in check and live a Modest life. While such a behavior may be seen as exceptional, one still maintains the position of their lower status.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
The bird meets with misfortune through flying.

The conditions are not favorable for one to embark at this time.

### Six in Second
She passes by her ancestor and meets her ancestress. He does not reach his prince and meets the official. No blame.

One seeks to perform their duty, but conditions of the time prevent Union with the correct superiors. No repercussions develop should one instead engage with another who shares a greater degree of mutual solidarity, peer or otherwise.

### Nine in Third
If one is not extremely careful, somebody may come up from behind and strike him. Misfortune.

Should one advance without accounting for their actions or surroundings, they may inspire secret enmity in their peers and be galvanized to attack. This negative development is rooted in overconfidence.

### Nine in Fourth
No blame. He meets him without passing by. Going brings danger. One must be on guard. Do not act. Be constantly persevering.

The situation has come about due to the nature of the times. In this time, one cannot meet with their superior. Trying to meet with them puts one in a dangerous spot, as exposing oneself in this way shall make them the target of far greater dangers and threats. One must exercise Resoluteness of the highest degree to avoid any action or involvement.

### Six in Fifth
Dense clouds, no rain from our western territory. The prince shoots and hits him who is in the cave.

One is unable to carry out an undertaking due to lack of support. Help may be found in even the darkest places so long as they are capable for the task.

### Six in Sixth
He passes him by, not meeting him. The flying bird leaves him. Misfortune. This means bad luck and injury.

One in their overconfidence passes by the opportunity to undertake something great. Missing out on this, and future opportunities, is quite injurious.
