# Hexagram 15 - Modesty (Ch'ien)

## Overview

### Judgement
Modesty creates success. The superior man carries things through.

Like a pillar reaching towards the heavens, mountains support the Earth, distributing the blessings of rain to nourish life in valleys and rivers below. Over time, mountains descend to meet the plains that rise to them, symbolizing how the lofty are humbled and the humble are exalted. This natural process mirrors the superior individual's role in human society, balancing extremes to address social discontent.

### The Image
Within the earth, a mountain: the image of Modesty. Thus the superior man reduces that which is too much, and augments that which is too little. He weighs things and makes them equal.

Nature inherently follows cycles, where fullness gives way to modesty. Humans possess the distinctive capacity to act through perspectives of both service and self-interest. However, when one in a high position maintains a modest demeanor, they shine with wisdom. They become a beacon of influence and respect, proving that true leadership lies in humility.

### Secondary Names
- Humbling

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
A superior man modest about his modesty may cross the great water. Good fortune.

Adopting an unassuming attitude that naturally resolves issues with ease and without opposition from others is highly beneficial. This approach not only facilitates smooth interactions but also empowers one to undertake profound life changes, leading to good fortune by allowing one to navigate through life's challenges with grace and minimal resistance.

### Six in Second
Modesty that comes into expression. Perseverance brings good fortune.

When one's humility is so profound that it naturally manifests in their actions and demeanor, it becomes a wellspring of good fortune. This outward expression of modesty not only reflects inner virtue but also creates the opportunity for a lasting impact, as the quiet strength of such a character influences others over time through consistent, humble perseverance.

### Nine in Third
A superior man of modesty and merit carries things to conclusion. Good fortune.

An individual of high character, who remains modest despite their capabilities and status, is able to see tasks through to their end. This humility, not swayed by the allure of fame or wealth, allows them to work effectively for the greater good, bringing about successful outcomes that benefit not just themselves but also those they serve, thereby attracting good fortune.

### Six in Fourth
Nothing that would not further modesty in movement.

Success can be achieved through a humble approach to one's tasks. By serving as a supportive assistant to those in higher positions with modesty and by openly acknowledging the achievements of those below, one fosters an environment of collaboration and recognition. This modest conduct in action furthers both personal growth and collective success.

### Six in Fifth
No boasting of wealth before one's neighbor. It is favorable to attack with force. Nothing that would not further.

Leaders who embody modesty must actively use their influence to invigorate situations that have stagnated or to correct the missteps of their peers. They should not merely observe in silence, hoping for self-correction. Instead, with a humble yet assertive approach, they are encouraged to take decisive action to guide, motivate, or realign, ensuring progress and harmony. Such proactive engagement, devoid of arrogance, only serves to advance the common good.

### Six in Sixth
Modesty that comes into expression. It is favorable to set armies marching to chastise one's own city and one's country.

Achieving something significant requires the courage to take firm action against one's own community or nation when necessary. A genuinely modest leader must be prepared to discipline or correct those within their group when personal ambitions or conflicts threaten unity. This self-directed critique, executed with humility, ensures that the collective remains aligned with higher principles, fostering growth and reformation rather than division.
