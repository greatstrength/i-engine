# Hexagram 54 - The Marrying Maiden (Kuei Mei)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of the Marrying Maiden portrays the Eldest Son leading the Youngest Daughter in gladness. This hexagram describes the attitude that one ought to take when entering into a voluntary relationship with a superior. Such describes the nature of both marriage and employment for an undertaking.

### Judgement
The Marrying Maiden. Undertakings bring misfortune. Nothing that would further.

Embarking upon one’s whims and desires while in a voluntary relationship only serves to bring harm. One ought to maintain tactful reserve and unwavering devotion such that their actions do not upset the existing order of things.

### The Image
Thunder over the lake: the image of the Marrying Maiden. Thus the superior man understands the transitory in the light of the eternity of the end.

One of enduring character understands that nature is transitory and that changing times bring new opportunities for growth. Voluntary relationships like this shall serve to bring opportunities for growth in the meantime and shall end of their own accord in time.

### Secondary Names
- Converting the Maiden

### Key Terms
- **Transform**:
  - Reveal hidden potential, turn into
  - Return to yourself or where you belong
  - Restore, revert, convert
  - Loyal
  - Give a young woman in marriage
  - A wife arriving to become a mistress

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
The marrying maiden as a concubine. A lame man who is able to tread. Undertakings bring good fortune.

One is accepted into the confidence of a prominent superior. So long as they understand their place in the established order, they may be called to carry out tasks by the superior, even if they are not entirely qualified to do so.

### Nine in Second
A one-eyed man who is able to see. The perseverance of a solitary man furthers.

One devotes themselves to a cause in which the present leadership presents itself as inept, leading to disappointment. Despite the setback, one continues on for the cause nonetheless.

### Six in Third
The marrying maiden as a slave. She marries as a concubine.

One seeks joy and advantage that rests beyond their station. To acquire them in this way, one must sacrifice their dignity and accept their allotted position, even if they think it to be beneath them.

### Nine in Fourth
The marrying maiden draws out the allotted time. A late marriage comes in due course.

One, despite the recommendations of others, does not throw themselves into a voluntary relationship, as there are none that suit them. Should they be patient, an opportunity that matches their character shall arrive in time.

### Six in Fifth
The sovereign I gave his daughter in marriage. The embroidered garments of the princess were not as gorgeous as those of the serving maid. The moon that is nearly full brings good fortune.

One of higher rank enters into a voluntary relationship with a leader of lower social standing. Despite the differences apparent, one who respects their subservience in the voluntary relationship despite their intrinsic social standing shall serve to benefit things.

### Six in Sixth
The woman holds the basket, but there are no fruits in it. The man stabs the sheep, but no blood flows. Nothing that acts to further.

One finds themselves in an empty voluntary relationship. While such a relationship exists in concept, there is nothing to gain from it with "no fruits" and no intrinsic guiding spirit or "blood". Such a superficial relationship shall not last long.
