# Hexagram 64 - Before Completion (Wei Chi)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Fire over Water, depicts a transition from chaos toward order, where a new situation approaches formation yet remains unstable, lacking a steady equilibrium. Caution is essential at this juncture. Hasty actions driven by impatience or inner unrest, akin to a young fox dipping its tail in the water just before finishing a crossing, risk undermining progress and invite regret. Deliberation and measured steps are required to ensure success, emphasizing the need to navigate this delicate phase with care to secure a favorable outcome.

### Judgement
Before Completion. Success. But if the little fox, after nearly completing the crossing, gets his tail in the water, there is nothing that would further.

### The Image
Fire over water: the image of the condition before transition. Thus the superior man is careful in the differentiation of things, so that each finds its place.

This image presents fire rising above water falling, symbolizing a state on the verge of transition yet marked by inherent tension and instability. One of inner strength, observing this dynamic, exercises diligence in distinguishing the elements and actors within a situation. Through this careful differentiation, they address the underlying uncertainty by ensuring each component is properly understood and positioned, allowing events to unfold as intended and facilitating a steady progression toward completion.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
He gets his tail in the water. Humiliating.

When times of chaos turn into times of order, the temptation arises to act quickly to accomplish something tangible before others. As one has not taken any precautions before advancing, their actions are met with opposition and a damage in trust.

### Nine in Second
He brakes his wheels. Perseverance brings good fortune.

One refrains from advancing, realizing that the times do not favor advance. Contending oneself to Waiting in preparation serves to benefit until a suitable time for advancement has presented itself.

### Six in Third
Before completion, attack brings misfortune. It furthers one to cross the great water.

This line warns that any outward action or forward motion at this stage will be premature, resulting in unfavorable consequences due to the situation’s incomplete state. Full preparedness has not yet been attained, rendering one’s readiness inadequate for decisive or public engagement. A fundamental shift or transformation—symbolized by "crossing the great water"—remains necessary to ensure progress, highlighting the importance of patience and continued internal or strategic refinement to align with the imminent transition.

### Nine in Fourth
Perseverance brings good fortune. Remorse disappears. Shock, thus to discipline the Devil’s Country. For three years, great realms are awarded.

One advances Resolutely, as the time for action has come. However, chaos has engrained itself deeply in the situation, requiring tremendous effort to dislodge. Should one engage in the extended work of dislodging it, there shall be much to be thankful for.

### Six in Fifth
Perseverance brings good fortune. No remorse. The light of the superior man is true. Good fortune.

One achieves victory over the situation, leaving no room for potential misgivings. The work of such a person is genuine and serves to benefit further.

### Nine in Sixth
There is drinking of wine in genuine confidence. No blame. But if one wets his head, he loses it, in truth.

One may partake in indulgences without repercussion so long as they maintain appropriate Conduct. Should one lose themselves to mirth, they too risk losing their credibility.
