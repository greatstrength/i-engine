# Hexagram 60 - Limitation (Chieh)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram of Limitation, with Water over Lake, portrays water entering an already full lake, causing it to overflow. This imagery reflects the natural boundaries inherent in all things, where limits define capacity and order. Despite the fluid nature of both trigrams, the lake’s overflow underscores the necessity of measured restraint to maintain harmony and foster progress.

### Judgement
Limitation. Success. Galling limitation must not be persevered in.

Limitation is inherent in nature, however limiting oneself arbitrarily or for the sake of drill is not productive and ought to be avoided.

### The Image
Water over the lake: the image of Limitation. Thus the superior man creates number and measure, and examines the nature of virtue and correct conduct.

All things serve a role in nature. Thus a leader differentiates between like and unlike things to bring orderly limits to the world.

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Not going out of the door and the courtyard is without blame.

One faces the beginnings of an understanding only to find themselves limited to succeed. Therefore, maintaining discretion about one’s plans bears no ill repercussions.

### Nine in Second
Not going out of the gate and the courtyard brings misfortune.

It is important for one to abide by the limits of the times, especially when the situation is currently not yet ripe. Yet should an opportunity to act be limited due to hesitation, this inner instability begets negative developments.

### Six in Third
He who knows no limitation will have cause to lament. No blame.

One without restraint is likened to bring humiliation to themselves through their excessive actions. Should they recognize their role and take accountability for their actions, they may redeem themselves.

### Six in Fourth
Contented limitation. Success.

When one expresses limitation innocently in their lives, it brings both satisfaction and success to one’s character.

### Nine in Fifth
Sweet limitation brings good fortune. Going brings success.

When one is able to provide limitation to themselves while gently promoting others to do the same, others shall follow their example of their own accord should they be aligned.

### Six in Sixth
Galling limitation. Perseverance brings misfortune. Remorse disappears.

Severe limitation is violent in nature and ought not be endured for its own sake. Only when one is in sufficient danger in which motion has been brought to a halt through irresolution should such severe limitation be used only as a means to restore motion.
