# Hexagram 25 - Innocence (Wu Wang)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Heaven above Thunder, symbolizes an individual whose actions are driven by a divine or pure purpose. When actions are taken innocently, with the intention of serving the greater good, they are devoid of selfish or darker motives. This state of innocence fosters a natural alignment with the cosmic order, promoting growth and authenticity in one's endeavors.

### Judgement
Innocence, supreme success. Perseverance furthers. If someone is not as they should be, he has misfortune, and it does not further him to undertake anything.

The essence of the soul is growth, and one's actions serve as mirrors for self-refinement. Deeds performed with innocence are naturally blessed, but any action tainted by ulterior motives will lack fortune and lead to misfortune. Acting in ways contrary to one's true nature invites negative outcomes.

### The Image
Under heaven thunder rolls: all things attain the natural state of innocence. Thus the kings of old, rich in virtue, and in harmony with the time, fostered and nourished all beings.

The first thunderstorms of summer symbolize a period of creative flourishing, akin to the purity and growth associated with child-like innocence. Such a spirit holds power among people, emphasizing the critical role of leaders in not only nurturing their own creative spirit but also in cultivating this spirit within their people. Like thunderstorms that invigorate the land, leaders must inspire and foster creativity and innocence in those around them for the collective benefit and growth of society.

### Secondary Names
- The Unexpected
- Without Embroiling

### Key Terms

- **Entangle**:

## Changing Lines

### Nine in First
Innocent behavior brings good fortune.

The initial impulses or instincts of the heart are inherently good. It's crucial to trust and follow these natural inclinations with confidence, as doing so will lead to good fortune. This line emphasizes the value of acting from a place of purity and authenticity, where the simplicity and sincerity of one's actions align with the natural order, bringing about positive outcomes.

### Six in Second
If one does not count the harvest while plowing, nor on the use of the ground while clearing it, it furthers one to undertake something.

This line advises that one should engage in activities purely for the sake of the activity itself, without fixating on the outcomes or benefits. By focusing on the task at hand with a mindset of innocence and simplicity, all endeavors are more likely to succeed. This approach fosters a state of being where actions are performed with genuine intent and presence, leading to naturally fruitful results.

### Six in Third
Undeserved misfortune. The cow that was tethered by someone is the wanderer's gain, the citizen's loss.

This line describes a scenario where innocent or well-intentioned actions inadvertently lead to misfortune. Here, a cow, symbolizing something valuable or beneficial, is lost by its rightful owner (the citizen) due to unintended consequences of others' actions, only to be gained by someone else (the wanderer). This misfortune is not deserved but is a result of the circumstances or timing. The advice is to accept such losses with an understanding that there might be a larger, perhaps unfathomable, plan or pattern at play, encouraging one to move forward without bitterness but with a recognition of life's inherent complexities and injustices.

### Nine in Fourth
He who can be persevering remains without blame.

By steadfastly adhering to what is rightfully theirs and not striving for more than what is due, one avoids creating scenarios where they might be held accountable or blamed. This line emphasizes the virtue of contentment and integrity, suggesting that perseverance in one's rightful path or with one's true possessions, without greed or overreach, leads to a life free from fault or moral blame.

### Nine in Fifth
Use no medicine in an illness incurred through no fault of your own. It will pass of itself.

When one faces misfortune or affliction that comes from external sources and is not a result of one's own actions or decisions, this line advises against active intervention or seeking remedies. The suggestion is to let such conditions resolve naturally, trusting in the process of time and the natural order to heal or rectify the situation. It reflects the idea that some problems, especially those not rooted in one's own doing, will dissolve on their own without the need for forced action, promoting patience and faith in natural recovery.

### Nine in Sixth
Innocent action brings misfortune. Nothing furthers.

In this scenario, acting with innocence or naivety, pushing forward against the natural flow or the limits set by fate, leads to misfortune. The insistence on pursuing a path or action, despite signs that it's not the right time or approach, will not yield success. This line warns that even well-intentioned or pure-hearted efforts, when misaligned with the timing or circumstances dictated by the larger forces at play, can be counterproductive. It's a reminder that one should not expend energy, regardless of the purity of intent, in ways that go against the natural order or current of destiny.
