# Hexagram 18 - Decay (Ku)

## Overview

### Summary
The trigrams of Mountain over Wind depict a scenario of stagnation, where this decay is not a natural outcome but results from human culpability. This decay arises when gentle, perhaps too lenient, forces below meet with an unyielding, stubborn resistance from above. The presence of this hexagram serves as both a warning and an urgent call to action, indicating that only the correct and timely application of energy can reverse the situation.

The decay here is a consequence of the misuse of freedoms, leading to a state of entropy. To revive what has been spoiled, one must act decisively yet with full awareness, understanding not only the necessary corrective actions but also when to begin and when to conclude these efforts, all while being vigilant against the risk of regression. This hexagram underscores the importance of timing, strategy, and mindfulness in overcoming decay, suggesting that with proper management, great transformation and success are possible.

### Judgement
Work on What Has Been Spoiled has supreme success. It furthers one to cross the great water. Before the starting point, three days. After the starting point, three days.

Decay that stems from the abuse of human freedom cannot be fixed immediately, as it takes time for situations to devolve to such a state. In this way, one must be willing to completely and irreversibly adjust themselves, as suggested by crossing the great water, to address these abuses with the appropriate prescriptive action. Yet such action ought not be committed to right away. One must take the time to thoroughly understand their predicament such that they may arrive at the appropriate set of actions (three days before) and then wait and observe the outcomes of such actions to ensure their success in addressing the decay (three days after).

### The Image
The wind blows low on the mountain: the image of Decay. Thus the superior man stirs up the people and strengthens their spirit.

The act of addressing decay is one that, despite starting difficultly, can invigorate the spirit, as the removal of blockages and the establishment of new habits has the impact of improving both one's mood and productivity. By seizing the moment’s flow, one can rouse others into action, channeling their energy to tackle the rot. By drawing them in, one not only halts decay but lifts their spirit, forging strength through shared purpose.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
Setting right what has been spoiled by the father. If there is a son, no blame rests upon the departed father. Danger. In the end good fortune.

The decay present is a result of an overly rigid adherence to outdated traditions or methods, symbolized by the "father." It's not too late for someone to recognize this and take steps towards correction. Acknowledging the peril associated with each reformative action, one can still proceed with necessary changes. This line suggests that by confronting the dangers head-on, one can achieve good fortune in the end, absolving the legacy of the past ("the departed father") through proactive, courageous action to mend what has been damaged or neglected.

### Nine in Second
Setting right what has been spoiled by the mother. One must not be too persevering.

The decay here stems from a weakness in character, perhaps an overly emotional or nurturing approach that has gone awry, symbolized by the "mother." Addressing this type of decay requires a sensitive and gentle approach rather than stubborn perseverance. The lesson is to correct these emotional or nurturing mistakes with understanding and care, rather than with force or excessive determination, suggesting that sometimes, a softer, more considerate method is needed to heal and restore balance.

### Nine in Third
Setting right what has been spoiled by the father. There will be a little remorse. No great blame.

One approaches the task of correcting past errors, those inherited from a rigid or traditional "father" figure, with excessive zeal. While this might lead to some missteps or cause minor annoyances, the overall intent to repair and improve is recognized. Thus, although there might be some regret or small faults along the way, the effort does not incur significant blame, highlighting that the desire to mend what has been spoiled, even if done with too much vigor, is still valued and seen as a step towards rectification.

### Six in Fourth
Tolerating what has been spoiled by the father. In continuing one sees humiliation.

If one is too weak or unwilling to confront the decay that has resulted from past rigidities or traditions (the "father"), allowing it to persist will lead to humiliation. This line warns that passivity or tolerance in the face of decay not only perpetuates the problem but also diminishes one's own standing or dignity. It suggests that facing and addressing inherited decay is necessary to avoid the shame that comes from letting negative patterns continue unchecked.

### Six in Fifth
Setting right what has been spoiled by the father. One meets with praise.

By confronting and actively working to correct the mistakes or decay inherited from past traditions or authority figures ("the father"), one earns commendation. This action not only addresses the decay but also mobilizes others (helpers) towards improvement, showing leadership and initiative. The praise comes from recognizing the effort and courage it takes to challenge established, perhaps flawed, systems or practices, leading to positive change and collective effort towards betterment.

### Nine in Sixth
He does not serve kings and princes, sets himself higher goals.

Upon recognizing that the current state of affairs has deteriorated beyond repair, one chooses to withdraw from direct involvement in the corrupted systems of power, symbolized by "kings and princes." This retreat is not out of despair but from a vision for a better future. Like a great sage, one steps back to reflect, plan, and prepare for a new era or way of being for humanity. This line celebrates the wisdom of setting one's sights on loftier, more lasting goals, acknowledging that sometimes the most profound contributions come not from engagement with the flawed present but from envisioning and working towards a renewed, higher purpose.
