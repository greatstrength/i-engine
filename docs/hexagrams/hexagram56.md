# Hexagram 56 - The Wanderer (Lü)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Fire over Mountain, portrays a fire burning through grass along a mountainside, wandering without lingering. It reflects the transient nature of a sojourner in unfamiliar lands, where success lies in maintaining clarity and modesty while navigating temporary circumstances. One must act with restraint, focusing on small matters to avoid drawing undue attention, ensuring safe passage through foreign environments.

### Judgement
The Wanderer. Success through smallness. Perseverance brings good fortune to the wanderer.

The trigrams of Fire on the Mountain portray a fire burning through grass along the mountainside. As it burns, it wanders and does not tarry. So too does one of enduring character apply clearness of mind and firmness of character when deciding matters of lesser import.

### The Image
Fire on the mountain: the image of The Wanderer. Thus the superior man is clear-minded and cautious in imposing penalties, and protracts no lawsuits.

One who wanders through strange lands does well to support themselves while not drawing too much attention through their actions. Doing so leads to positive developments.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
If the wanderer busies himself with trivial things, he draws down misfortune upon himself.

One who wanders in an alien environment ought not get involved with local matters that do not concern them. Continued involvement in this way leads to negative developments.

### Six in Second
The wanderer comes to an inn. He has his property with him. He wins the steadfastness of a young servant.

One who wanders in an alien environment that carries an attitude of Modesty shall be helped. Should one need a place to stay, provisions, or even help, the locals shall provide.

### Nine in Third
The wanderer’s inn burns down. He loses the steadfastness of his young servant. Danger.

One who is in a foreign land may bring trouble should they be quick to anger. They shall lose any help and bring danger to their situation.

### Nine in Fourth
The wanderer rests in a shelter. He obtains his property and an ax. My heart is not glad.

One who is in a foreign land acquires temporary means through their Modesty. Yet despite the circumstance, they remain on guard with an uneasy heart.

### Six in Fifth
He shoots a pheasant. It drops with the first arrow. In the end this brings both praise and office.

One who seeks office in a foreign land demonstrates their capabilities with great alacrity by shooting the "pheasant," an offering of support to a superior, with the "first arrow." Such a demonstration of skill is recognized and positive developments result.

### Six in Sixth
The bird’s nest burns up. The wanderer laughs at first, then must needs lament and weep. Through carelessness he loses his cow. Misfortune.

One who travels in a foreign land sacrifices their Modesty in favor of reckless and unbecoming behavior. While fun at first, they lose their place to stay and attract the ire of the locals, leading to negative developments.
