# Hexagram 6 - Conflict (Sung)

## Overview

### Summary
Conflict emerges from the core of uncertainty, often stemming from a lack of preparation or foresight. To mitigate such disputes, one must consider all aspects thoroughly from the outset.

### Judgement
Conflict. You are sincere and are being obstructed. A cautious halt halfway brings good fortune. Going through to the end brings misfortune. It furthers one to see the great man. It furthers one to cross the great water.

Individuals who are both inwardly cunning and outwardly resolute tend to be naturally contentious. When engaged in conflict, the wise approach is to seek a middle ground, aiming for resolution rather than prolonging the battle, which only leads to mutual harm.

### The Image
Heaven and water go their opposite ways: the image of Conflict. Thus in all his transactions the superior man carefully considers the beginning.

Initiating new endeavors during times of conflict is ill-advised; success in such periods is more likely when there's unity rather than division. It's beneficial to involve an experienced mediator or judge, someone who can offer a fair and impartial perspective to resolve the conflict more amicably, highlighting the value of wisdom and objectivity in navigating disputes.

### Secondary Names
- Arguing

### Key Terms
- **Conflict/Argument**:
  - Dispute, plead your case, demand justice, contend in front of the ruler or judge
  - Lodge a complaint, begin litigation
  - Quarrels, wrangles, controversy
  - Correct, reprimand, arrive at judgment, resolve a conflict

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
If one does not perpetuate the affair, there is a little gossip. In the end good fortune comes.

When facing a superior adversary at the very outset of a conflict, the wise course is to avoid escalating the situation. By stepping back, the dispute can be reduced to mere gossip, something minor and soon forgotten. This restraint leads to a fortunate resolution, demonstrating that sometimes, the best strategy in conflict is to minimize engagement, allowing peace to prevail through non-confrontation.

### Nine in Second
One cannot engage in conflict; one returns home, gives way. The people in his town, three hundred households, remain free of blame.

In moments of facing a superior adversary, it's vital for one to consider those dependent on them. Choosing to step back from the fight preserves not only one's own integrity but also safeguards the community, represented by the three hundred households. There is no dishonor in retreat when it ensures the well-being and blamelessness of many, highlighting the virtue of choosing peace over confrontation.

### Six in Third
To nourish oneself on ancient virtue induces perseverance. Danger. In the end, good fortune comes. If by chance you seek service of a king, seek not works.

Even when one might feel diminished while serving others, there's an inherent growth in wisdom and merit that comes from such experiences, which cannot be taken away. Drawing from the well of ancient virtue, one learns to endure through challenges. Despite the dangers or feelings of injustice, persistence leads to a fortunate outcome, reminding one that true value lies in the character and lessons gained, not merely in the recognition or tasks performed.

### Nine in Fourth
One cannot engage in conflict. One turns back and submits to fate, changes one's attitude, and finds peace in perseverance. Good fortune.

At first, one might be driven by the desire to assert oneself, even at the expense of others. However, one's higher qualities or moral compass intervene, leading to a surrender to the natural flow of events. By altering one's approach and embracing perseverance, peace and good fortune emerge, teaching that sometimes, the greatest strength lies in yielding to fate rather than fighting against it.

### Nine in Fifth
To contend before him brings supreme good fortune.

One should seek out a judge who will uphold justice according to the circumstances. Those who align with what is right and just will see significant benefits from this decision, illustrating the power of having disputes resolved by an impartial and fair authority.

### Nine in Sixth
Even if by chance a leather belt is bestowed on one, by the end of a morning it will have been snatched away three times.

One might achieve victory by pushing the conflict to its extreme conclusion. However, any accolades or honors received are fleeting, as one continually finds themselves embroiled in further disputes. This line suggests that winning through to the bitter end might not bring peace or lasting recognition but rather a cycle of ongoing conflict, highlighting the transient nature of such 'victories'.
