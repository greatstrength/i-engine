# Hexagram 59 - Dispersion (Huan)

## Overview

### Summary
As the wind blows the foam off the surface of the water, so too do the winds of circumstance clear away the impediments of flow to life energies. It is a time of gentle break-ups. When the Wind Blows over the Water, it dissipates it into foam and mist. So too can an outward gentleness be used to break up a blockage of life energies in a situation.

### Judgement
Dispersion. Success. The king approaches his temple. It furthers one to cross the great water. Perseverance furthers.

Success from Dispersion comes from the breakup of divisive egotism in one’s group and amongst their followers. In this way, one may employ the necessary means of gentleness through acts of ritual purification and communal celebration. Should a leader persevere in such an attitude towards their followers, they shall create a communal bond that is capable of accomplishing great things.

### The Image
The wind drives over the water: the image of Dispersion. Thus the kings of old sacrificed to the Lord and built temples.

Harshness and selfishness create a rigidity within one's spirit that can isolate them from their purpose and their community. In this way, communities create spiritual rituals and celebrations to remind the individuals of the community of their roots and the greater purpose at hand.

### Secondary Names
- Dissolution

### Key Terms
- **Disperse**:
  - Scatter clouds, break up obstacles, evaporate
  - Dispel illusions, fears, and suspicions
  - Clear things up, dissolve resistance
  - Untie, separate
  - Change and mobilize what is rigid
  - Melting ice, floods, fog lifting and clearing away
  - Changing form through expanding and scattering

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
He brings help with the strength of a horse. Good fortune.

Should rigid egotism make itself present within the community, it ought to be dealt with resolutely with the "strength of a horse." In this way, any misunderstandings may be resolved, leading to positive developments.

### Nine in Second
At the dissolution he hurries to that which supports him. Remorse disappears.

Should one become alienated from their community, withdrawing to the people and situations that more naturally align with them shall remove all cause for regret.

### Six in Third
He dissolves his self. No remorse.

One finds themselves in a situation where rigid egotism must be dissolved out of practical necessity. There is no cause for regret.

### Six in Fourth
He dissolves his bond with his group. Supreme good fortune. Dispersion leads in turn to accumulation. This is something that ordinary men do not think of.

One finds themselves in a group or community to which they do not belong. Thus they remove themselves. This is a positive development on all counts, as the discarding of the outmoded makes way for new possibilities. People who are of the nature to follow do not consider the possibility of excusing themselves from the group in this way out of fear of the unknown. Such people would rather abide in the comfort of a group to which they don't entirely belong than risk isolation.

### Nine in Fifth
His loud cries are dissolving as sweat. Dissolution! A king abides without blame.

There are times when, during times of gridlock or prolonged stress, a profound idea emerges. As is during the height of a fever, this idea vigorously dissolves the gridlock by creating a central focus around which the community may congregate. Such is the profundity of the idea that a superior of the highest authority enforces it with no complaint.

### Nine in Sixth
He dissolves his blood. Departing, keeping at a distance, going out, is without blame.

When one feels that their association with a particular group or community may bring potential harm to their person or followers, they do no harm to themselves by keeping their distance by any means.
