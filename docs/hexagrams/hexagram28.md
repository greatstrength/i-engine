# Hexagram 28 - Preponderance of the Great (Ta Kuo)

## Overview

### Summary
This hexagram, with Lake above Tree, signifies a critical juncture where extreme conditions demand extraordinary responses. It reflects a time when structures, whether personal, social, or material, are strained to their breaking point, necessitating bold and transformative action. The collapse of old frameworks clears the path for new beginnings, provided one approaches these challenges with resilience, clarity, and a focus on higher purpose.

### Judgement
The ridgepole sags to the breaking point. It furthers to have somewhere to go. Success.

This hexagram signifies a critical juncture where extreme conditions demand extraordinary responses. It calls for a deep understanding of the underlying significance of these potentially transformative times, where the collapse of old structures under their own weight clears the way for new constructions.

### The Image
The lake rises above the trees: the image of Preponderance of the Great. Thus the superior man, when he stands alone, is unconcerned, and if he has to renounce the world, he is undaunted.

The imagery of the Tree under the Lake suggests an approach one should adopt during such pivotal moments. This attitude combines the steadfastness of a tree with the joy one can find in solitude, enabling one to face the monumental challenges of the era with resilience and without fear, even if it means standing or proceeding alone.

### Secondary Names
- Great Exceeding

### Key Terms
- **Great**:
  - Big, noble, important
  - Able to protect others
  - Orient one's will toward a self-imposed goal
  - The ability to lead or guide one's life
  - Yang energy
- **Excess**:
  - Go beyond
  - Pass by, pass over, surpass
  - Overtake, overshoot
  - Get clear of, get over
  - Cross the threshold, surmount difficulties
  - Transgress the norms, outside the limits
  - Too much

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
To spread with rushes underneath. No blame.

During times of exceptional challenge or when facing monumental changes, one should act with extreme caution. The metaphor of laying rushes underneath suggests preparing with extra care, perhaps even to an extent that might seem excessive. This caution, however, does not lead to blame but is rather seen as a prudent approach, ensuring that actions taken do not exacerbate the precariousness of the situation but instead lay a foundation for stability or recovery.

### Nine in Second
A dry poplar sprouts at the root. An older man takes a young wife. Everything furthers.

This line symbolizes a period of extraordinary renewal and growth amidst challenging times. The image of a dry poplar sprouting at the root signifies that even what appears barren or past its prime can rejuvenate and flourish. The analogy of an older man marrying a young wife illustrates an unconventional but fruitful union or partnership, suggesting that in times of great change, unexpected or seemingly unusual alliances or situations can lead to positive outcomes.

### Nine in Third
The ridgepole sags to the breaking point. Misfortune.

This line describes a situation where one stubbornly pushes forward or tries to maintain the status quo in the face of overwhelming change or warning signs. Like a ridgepole that is about to collapse under its own weight, the individual's persistence leads to disaster. Ignoring the advice, support, or the natural flow of events results in a lack of assistance when it's most needed, culminating in misfortune due to an inability or unwillingness to adapt or let go of what is no longer sustainable.

### Nine in Fourth
The ridgepole is braced. Good fortune. If there are ulterior motives, it is humiliating.

In this scenario, someone in a position of responsibility or leadership receives support from an individual of lesser status to navigate through the challenging times, much like bracing a sagging ridgepole to prevent collapse. This assistance leads to good fortune, as it helps stabilize or restore what was at risk. However, the relationship must remain pure in intent. If the one receiving help uses this support for selfish gain or ulterior motives, the outcome turns to humiliation, as the trust and goodwill that facilitated the aid are undermined, leading to a loss of integrity and respect.

### Nine in Fifth
A withered poplar puts forth flowers. An older woman takes a husband. No blame. No praise.

This line illustrates a choice made out of fear or preference for the familiar during times of upheaval, where one opts for comfort over growth or potential, akin to choosing an older, less fecund wife over a younger one. While this decision incurs no immediate blame, as it's a personal choice, it also garners no praise because it's an "empty action" in terms of long-term benefits or progress. This choice may stabilize the present but at the cost of hindering future growth or opportunities, suggesting a stagnation or missed potential in the broader scope of life or change.

### Six in Sixth
One must go through the water. It goes over one's head. Misfortune. No blame.

This line describes a situation where one's dedication to their principles or path leads them into overwhelming circumstances, symbolized by being submerged in water. The decision to "go through the water" even when it leads to misfortune reflects an action taken out of conviction or necessity rather than recklessness. Despite the misfortune that befalls them, there is no blame, as the action was undertaken with innocence or pure intent, acknowledging the individual's commitment to their beliefs or duties, even in the face of adversity. This conveys that while the outcome is unfortunate, the moral integrity or innocence behind the action absolves one from fault.
