# Hexagram 44 - Coming to Meet (Kou)

## Overview

### Summary
The hexagram, with Heaven above Wind, represents the fifth month of the year, from June to July, when darkness begins to creep back into the land just after the solstice. It signifies a delicate interplay between strong and weak forces, where intentions shape the outcome of encounters, urging caution and discernment to foster growth while guarding against hidden motives.

### Judgement
Coming to Meet. The maiden is powerful. One should not marry such a maiden.

The union between strong and weak forces, when done with good intentions, may herald times of growth. However, should a weak force approach a strong one with hidden ambitions of power, a leader ought to be wary of malevolent intention. Turning a blind eye to followers like this allows them to gain positions of power not suited to them.

### The Image
Under heaven, wind: the image of Coming to Meet. Thus does the prince act when disseminating his commands and proclaiming them to the four quarters of heaven.

Like the Wind under Heaven, one ought to be gentle and penetrating in their desires with how they wish to see the world.

## Changing Lines

### Six in First
It must be checked with a brake of bronze. Perseverance brings good fortune. If one lets it take its course, one experiences misfortune. Even a lean pig has it in him to rage around.

A leader ought not underestimate the power of the follower that approaches and has found its way in. Perseverance in checking the followers brings good outcomes. Should a leader leave their followers be, there shall be trouble.

### Nine in Second
There is a fish in the tank. No blame. Does not further guests.

There is nothing to fear should the leader keep a watchful eye on the followers. However, should that leader go away, the weak nature of the followers returns. This may be particularly dangerous should a leader have outside guests present.

### Nine in Third
There is no skin on his thighs, and walking comes hard. If one is mindful of the danger, no great mistake is made.

There is temptation for a leader to fall in with the wrong followers, but time does not permit it. While this leads to painful indecision for the moment, the leader may walk away without ill repercussion should they be aware of their situation and keep their composure.

### Nine in Fourth
No fish in the tank. This leads to misfortune.

A leader ought to tolerate their followers such that they may be available when the time comes to undertake something. Yet should the leader not keep their followers well-disposed and subsequently lose them, the misfortune falls upon the leader.

### Nine in Fifth
A melon covered with willow leaves. Hidden lines. Then it drops down to one from heaven.

A leader who both enables and protects their followers from harm shall be blessed by their help.

### Nine in Sixth
He comes to meet with his horns. Humiliation. No blame.

A leader has chosen to walk the path of the sage. As they rebuff the world, they are perceived by their followers as arrogant and disconnected. Despite this humiliation, no ill repercussions stem from this.
